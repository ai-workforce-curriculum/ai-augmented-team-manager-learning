# mod-101 — The AI-Augmented Team Manager: Role, Scope, and the Human/Machine Contract

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours.

## What this module does

Defines the **level-40 team manager** scope in the AI Workforce family — a single team of 5–30 ICs whose daily work is AI-augmented — and positions it against sibling tracks at every neighbouring level. Reads the four canonical manager-output texts (Grove, Fournier, Larson, Zhuo) as the *assumed baseline* this track layers AI-augmentation onto. Authors the first draft of the team's **human/machine contract** — what AI may draft, what humans must do before shipping, who is on the hook, what happens when the AI is wrong, what data the AI may see. Establishes a working **deferral rubric** for the neighbours (down to the governance analyst, sideways to the risk engineer and product manager, up to the enablement lead and higher). Sets the manager's own **AI-fluency bar**: enough to review and model, not enough to out-ship the team.

## Learning objectives

- Define the level-40 team manager's scope in the AI Workforce family — team-scale operating system for AI-augmented work (one team, 5–30 ICs) — against what `ai-enablement-lead` (level 45) owns org-wide, what `ai-engineering-manager` (level 50) adds for manager-of-managers scope, and what IC tracks (`ai-assisted-software-engineer` level 25, `ai-augmented-analyst` level 20) teach that this role inherits.
- Read the manager-output fundamentals (Grove *High Output Management*, Fournier *The Manager's Path*, Larson *An Elegant Puzzle*, Zhuo *The Making of a Manager*) as the assumed baseline this track layers AI-augmentation onto — not re-taught here.
- Author the team's human/machine contract — what humans own, what the AI owns, what a human reviewer must do before an AI artifact ships, where the manager is on the hook, where ICs are, where the vendor is — grounded in the "AI generates, engineers own" language observed across the 25-posting sample.
- Position the ownership rule against sibling tracks — when to send a question down to `ai-governance-analyst` (level 15), sideways to `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25) / `ai-augmented-product-manager` (level 30), or up to `ai-enablement-lead` (level 45) / `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60).
- Build the manager's own AI-tool fluency bar — enough Cursor / Claude Code / Copilot / Promptfoo / LangSmith to review and model, not to out-ship ICs; documented against IAPP AIGP vocabulary for cross-functional work.

## Where this fits in the track

Mod-101 is the entry module. Every later module in the track takes one slice of what is sketched here and builds the working system for it. The forward links out of the chapters and exercises are the explicit map:

- Chapter 3 (human/machine contract) → `mod-103` encodes the C1 / C2 bar as standards-as-code; `mod-109` operates the C3 / C4 accountability + incident flow; `mod-110` folds C5 into governance translation.
- Chapter 4 (deferral rubric) → every subsequent module names the neighbours it links out to in the same language used here.
- Chapter 5 (fluency bar) → `mod-105` extends from manager-fluency to team-fluency; `mod-103` and `mod-106` use the reading-level eval and agent-authoring fluency practically.
