# Exercise 04 — Industry Survey Into Team Plan Translation

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours.

## Objective

Read the current round of industry surveys with the Chapter 4 discipline — surveys as input, not as plan — and produce the one-paragraph **industry-context section** of your exec pre-read, plus a short **divergence note** stating where your team roadmap disagrees with the baseline and why. The deliverable is small on purpose; the exercise is in the reading discipline, not the writing volume.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 4 of this module.
- Exercises 01 and 02.
- Access to the current editions of: Microsoft *Work Trend Index*, McKinsey *State of AI*, GitHub *Octoverse*, Stack Overflow *Developer Survey*. (Add a discipline-specific survey if your team is not engineering — see Chapter 4.)
- Access to at least two pieces of academic empirical research relevant to your team's work from among: Peng et al. on Copilot productivity; Noy & Zhang on writing; Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond on customer-service; Dell'Acqua et al. on consultants.

## Steps

1. **Skim the surveys for *methodology*, not the executive summary.** For each of the four named surveys, identify: the sample size, the sample population, the survey instrument (telemetry vs. self-report vs. mixed), the key definitions, and any year-on-year methodology changes. Ignore the press-release headline for now.
2. **Extract a small handful of findings relevant to your team.** Target: two findings per survey, max. Each finding is a direct quote or a two-sentence summary, with: the source, the reporting unit, and the range / point estimate. If the finding is a year-on-year change, record the prior year's number too.
3. **Run the four-question check on each finding.** For each finding: *who is in the sample; how is the key term defined; compared to what; does it match the other sources?* Reject findings that do not survive the check — they are hypotheses, not evidence.
4. **Compare to the academic empirical research.** Where the surveys agree with the empirical work (Peng et al., Dell'Acqua et al., etc.) directionally, upgrade the signal. Where the surveys and the empirical research disagree, note the disagreement explicitly.
5. **Cross-check against your Exercise 01 task sort.** For each of the surviving findings, ask: does this strengthen the sort, challenge the sort, or raise a specific question I have to answer in the pre-read? If "challenge," write down the specific line of the sort it challenges and the counter-reason.
6. **Draft the industry-context paragraph.** 120–180 words. Use *ranges*, not point estimates. Name the sources with dates. State the direction of each signal. Do not quote any single survey as authoritative.
7. **Draft the divergence note.** 60–120 words, two paragraphs or bullets. Paragraph 1: where our plan is *consistent* with the industry baseline, in one or two lines. Paragraph 2: where our plan *diverges*, with the reason. The divergence paragraph is the one that earns exec trust; do not skip it, and do not make it defensive.
8. **Attach a sources block.** Every quoted survey or paper, with its release date, the methodology link, and the specific figure or section referenced. The sources block is why the pre-read survives a follow-up question.

## Starter guidance

- The temptation is to read all four surveys cover-to-cover. Don't. 15 minutes per survey, methodology-first, then two findings, then move on. The rest can wait for a quarterly deep read (see Chapter 4's cadence).
- The executive summary of every one of these surveys is the loudest and least useful section. Read the demographic tables and the methodology appendix instead.
- A finding reported only in one survey is a hypothesis. Don't treat it as evidence until a second source agrees or the sample is independently credible.
- The academic empirical research is narrower but much better evidence per data point. Treat Peng et al., Dell'Acqua et al., Noy & Zhang, Brynjolfsson-Li-Raymond as "ground-truth-ish" where they overlap your team's work.
- "Aren't we behind?" and "aren't we ahead?" are the two questions the pre-read must defuse. Make sure the industry-context paragraph and the divergence note together answer both.
- The deliverable fits on half a page plus a sources block. If you write more, something belongs in a supporting doc.

## Acceptance criteria

The deliverable is complete when:

- Four surveys have been read methodology-first; two findings per survey have been extracted; each has been scored against the four-question check.
- At least two pieces of academic empirical research have been cross-referenced.
- Each surviving finding has been cross-checked against your Exercise 01 task sort with a note (*strengthens*, *challenges*, or *raises a question*).
- A 120–180-word industry-context paragraph uses *ranges*, not point estimates, with dated sources.
- A 60–120-word divergence note names at least one place where the team plan deviates from the industry baseline and gives the reason.
- A sources block lists every quoted survey / paper with release date, methodology link, and specific figure.
- Nothing in the paragraph is undated or unsourced.

## Stretch goals

- Write two versions of the industry-context paragraph: one for the exec pre-read, one for the team-channel note. Different audiences, same evidence; see which voice is harder.
- Pre-write the three questions you expect an exec to ask after reading the paragraph. For each, note which of your supporting docs answers it.
- Add a short "what we will re-read in Q2" line — the surveys you expect to update your view, and what you would change if they did.
- Translate the discipline-specific angle explicitly: if your team is not engineering, state which of the four surveys *does not* represent your team well, and which discipline-specific reference you are adding to compensate.
- Compare your 2026/2027 reading against the equivalent paragraph you would have written a year ago (if you have one). Year-on-year drift in the paragraph is itself useful signal.
