# Exercise 06 — Roadmap One-Pager and Exec Pre-Read

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours.

## Objective

Assemble the full artifact set — the **roadmap one-pager**, the **rollout decision log**, and the **exec pre-read "why this and not that" note** — for your team's current horizon, cross-linked and pinned. The pilot charters already exist from Exercise 02; this exercise closes the loop and produces the artifacts that will travel to the team, to partner functions, and to leadership. By the end of this exercise, every Chapter-1-through-5 decision has a written home, and the "why this and not that" paragraph is written in the voice of the question your VP will actually ask.

## Prerequisites

- All five prior chapters.
- Exercises 01–05.
- A target audience: your actual VP, skip-level, or partner-exec, named. Audience-specific vocabulary matters for the pre-read.
- Access to your team wiki or planning space so the artifacts can be pinned, not just written locally.

## Steps

1. **Assemble the roadmap one-pager.** Seven sections from Chapter 1: scope, task sort (from Exercise 01), sequence (from Exercise 02), adoption mode per step (from Exercise 03), success and kill criteria (from Exercise 02), partner coupling (from Exercise 05 and Chapter 5), decision-log stub. Fit on one legible page or two short ones. Ruthless cutting; supporting docs link out.
2. **Compose the rollout decision log.** One file, append-only. Back-fill the three-to-five decisions you have already made getting to this point (the task-sort scoring decisions, the pilot-skip call, the pilot/rollout sequencing, the adoption-mode calls, the graduation decision from Exercise 05). Each entry: date, owner, decision in one line, why in one-to-three lines, alternatives considered in one line, next review date if any.
3. **Draft the exec pre-read, section by section.** Seven sections from Chapter 6:
   - *One-sentence summary of the plan.*
   - *Industry context* — paste from Exercise 04.
   - *Risk framing* — blast radius from the task sort; kill criteria per pilot; the human-led bucket you are *not* automating and why.
   - *ROI framing* — one paragraph, team-level, honest about measurement maturity. (See [`mod-104`](../../mod-104-productivity-measurement-and-ai-roi/) for depth.)
   - *Why this and not that* — the alternatives you considered and did not pick. Two or three lines each. This is the section the exec will read most carefully; write it last, and rewrite it once.
   - *Partner couplings* — one paragraph from Chapter 5.
   - *What we are asking for* — the specific resource, coverage, signal, or decision from the reader. A pre-read with no ask is a status update.
4. **Cross-link the artifact set.** Roadmap one-pager links to pilot charters (Exercise 02), decision log, exec pre-read, human/machine contract ([`mod-101`](../../mod-101-ai-augmented-team-manager-position-and-contract/) Chapter 3), measurement dashboard. Pilot charter links to the roadmap step it serves and to the decision-log entry (even if blank for now). Decision log links back to the roadmap step. Exec pre-read links to the roadmap one-pager and the dashboard. Do the linking as you write; adding it after is almost always skipped.
5. **Run the "writing failure mode" pass.** Grep the artifacts for marketing voice ("innovative," "transformative," "cutting-edge"), hedging ("may consider," "aim to explore"), and un-dated / un-owned statements. Fix each.
6. **Reconcile against the human/machine contract.** For every task in the roadmap's task-sort section, check that the C1 bucket in the contract matches. If drift, fix the contract first (per [`mod-101`](../../mod-101-ai-augmented-team-manager-position-and-contract/) Chapter 3), then re-run the check.
7. **Pressure-test the pre-read against three exec questions.** Write a two-to-four-sentence response using the pre-read alone to each of:
   - *"Aren't we behind — WTI says 75% of workers use AI daily and we are only moving four workflows this quarter?"*
   - *"What's the cost if we are wrong on one of these pilots?"*
   - *"Why not mandate across the whole team, we did that for the SSO rollout?"*
   If the pre-read does not resolve any of the three, revise it.
8. **Pin and share.** Pin the roadmap one-pager in the team wiki and the exec share-point. Share the exec pre-read with the audience you named at step 0 with a 48-hour read window before the planned review. Share the roadmap one-pager with the team on the same day. Share the pilot charters with each cohort IC and each partner function.

## Starter guidance

- The one-pager lives or dies on page count. If it does not fit on one legible page, cut — move depth to the pilot charters, the decision log, or a supporting doc. Three pages reads as "did not think it through." One page reads as "is in command of the material."
- Write the "why this and not that" section *last* and rewrite it *once*. It is the differentiator; it deserves the second pass. A first draft that names its alternatives is good; a second pass that names them in the vocabulary of your specific VP is excellent.
- The decision log back-fill is often the most useful part of the exercise. Writing down the decisions you have already made surfaces the ones you did not actually make — and forces them into the open.
- The *What we are asking for* section is where pre-reads often fail by being vague. "Support for the plan" is not an ask. "Agreement that we hold mandate off this quarter and revisit Q2 against the opt-out adoption threshold" is an ask.
- The pre-read is written in *their* vocabulary, not yours. If the VP uses "throughput" and not "velocity," match. If the VP asks about cost every quarter, lead with cost framing in the ROI paragraph.
- The artifact set is not shipped; it is *pinned*. "Shipped" implies a one-time push. "Pinned" is the team-wiki posture that lets the artifacts travel and be edited as the quarter progresses.

## Acceptance criteria

The artifact set is complete when:

- The roadmap one-pager fits on one legible page (or two short pages), with all seven sections present, named owners throughout, dated decisions, no marketing voice and no hedging.
- The rollout decision log contains at least three back-filled entries with date, owner, decision, reasoning, and alternatives.
- The exec pre-read fits on one page with all seven sections present, including a "why this and not that" section with at least two alternatives named and reasoned.
- Cross-links are in place among all four artifact types: roadmap one-pager ↔ pilot charters ↔ decision log ↔ exec pre-read, and roadmap one-pager ↔ human/machine contract.
- The roadmap task-sort section is reconciled with the C1 bucket of the human/machine contract.
- The three exec questions each receive a two-to-four-sentence response grounded in the pre-read; any gaps have been fixed.
- The artifacts are pinned in the team wiki and the exec share-point; the pre-read has been shared with the named audience with a 48-hour read window.

## Stretch goals

- Walk the artifact set past a non-exec stakeholder — a peer manager, your PM peer, or a skip-level IC — before sending it to the exec. Fresh eyes catch marketing voice you have stopped seeing.
- Write the Q2 revision in draft now: what you expect to change, what you expect to carry through unchanged, and what decision triggers a mid-quarter revision. This primes the quarterly refresh cadence.
- For each item in the "why this and not that" section, add a *trigger-to-revisit* line: "we revisit mandate-mode if adoption > 70% *and* reviewer quality bar holds under opt-out for 6 weeks." Triggers turn alternatives into live options rather than closed debates.
- Record a 3-minute read-aloud of the exec pre-read and listen back. Marketing voice and hedging are easier to catch by ear than by eye.
- Post the roadmap one-pager (redacted of anything confidential) in a peer-manager channel or community of practice and solicit critique. The alternative paths peers surface are the material for next quarter's "why this and not that" section.
