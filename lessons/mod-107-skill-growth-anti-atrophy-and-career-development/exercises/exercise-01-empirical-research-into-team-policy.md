# Exercise 01 — Empirical Research Into Team Policy

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours.

## Objective

Produce the **research-to-policy translation memo** for your team: a 2–3-page artifact that reads the three research anchors ([Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161), [Noy & Zhang](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586), and [Dell'Acqua et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321)) at manager-fluency and translates each paper's load-bearing finding into **one named team-level policy move** with a single-sentence rationale that cites the source.

This is the first of the five mod-107 artifacts. Exercises 02, 03, 04, and 05 each build a downstream artifact that assumes the policy memo is in place; the memo is the artifact that justifies them to leadership, to peers, and to the team itself.

## Prerequisites

- [Chapter 1](../01-the-atrophy-question-and-why-this-module-exists.md) — the module's scope, the lagged-signal framing, and the R10 context.
- [Chapter 2](../02-brynjolfsson-li-raymond-and-the-growth-path-channel.md) — Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond at manager-fluency.
- [Chapter 3](../03-noy-and-zhang-and-the-differentiation-channel.md) — Noy & Zhang at manager-fluency.
- [Chapter 4](../04-dell-acqua-and-the-deference-channel.md) — Dell'Acqua et al. at manager-fluency.
- Access to the three primary sources (open-access or via your institution's library — all three are available in some form; see [`resources.md`](../resources.md)).
- A working view of your team: roles, seniority mix, recurring task types (as named in your mod-106 routing matrix if you have one).

## Problem statement

Every team in an AI-adopting organisation has an *implicit* theory of how AI is reshaping its skill base. The implicit theory lives in individual managers' heads, is rarely written down, and consequently rarely gets challenged or corrected. When the implicit theory is wrong — when it assumes AI is uniformly good for the team's capability and the growth channel takes care of itself — the atrophy work cannot begin.

The explicit memo is the response. It names, in writing, which research findings apply to your team's work, which policy moves the research justifies, and which caveats any of those moves carry. It is the document you can hand to your skip-level manager, to the HR partner, or to the senior ICs when they ask *"why are we doing the carve-outs?"* It is also the document you re-read next quarter when you tune the carve-outs and the ladder.

The memo is not a research paper. It is not a literature review. It is a *working manager's translation* — three findings, three policies, three sentences of rationale per policy, plus the caveats and the re-check date. Two to three pages. One author (you). One co-review.

## Steps

1. **Read each paper for the load-bearing finding.** Spend ~30 minutes per paper. Do not try to internalise everything; identify the single finding the chapter in this module anchors on and note the location (paper, section, approximate page) so your memo can cite it.
   - Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond — the *distributional* finding (gains concentrate at low tenure) and the workforce-dynamics channel.
   - Noy & Zhang — the *dispersion compression* finding.
   - Dell'Acqua et al. — the *uncritical consumer* finding on outside-frontier tasks (not the headline routing finding, which mod-106 anchors).
2. **Answer the three "does this apply to my team?" questions per paper.** For each paper, write two to three sentences answering:
   - *What task-types on my team does this finding most plausibly apply to?* (name specific task-types from your routing matrix or your team's rhythm)
   - *What signal would I expect to see if the finding is active on my team?* (something I could observe in 1:1s, retros, review outcomes, or the output comparison with the pre-AI baseline)
   - *What signal would tell me the finding is not applying here?* (specifically: what does disconfirmation look like, so the memo is falsifiable)
3. **Translate each finding into one policy move.** The policy move should be specific, scoped, and actionable within a quarter. Chapters 2, 3, and 4 each name candidate moves; pick the one that most applies to your team, and write a single sentence naming it. Examples (your team's version will be team-specific):
   - From Brynjolfsson: *"Rewrite the Day-30 junior-IC check-in to probe for re-derivation of at least one AI-assisted artifact."*
   - From Noy & Zhang: *"Amend the promotion-packet template to require a *judgement moves* section alongside the representative-artifact section."*
   - From Dell'Acqua: *"Add a paired-review rotation on the three outside-frontier task-types in the current routing matrix."*
4. **Write the rationale sentences.** For each policy move, write a one-sentence rationale that cites the paper (author + year + venue is sufficient — not a full citation) and names the finding. The rationale must survive a reader who challenges it: *"Noy & Zhang (Science, 2023) show AI compresses output-quality dispersion in realistic writing tasks, so our team's promotion signal from output volume is quieter than it was and the packet must surface the judgement moves that still differentiate."*
5. **Add the caveats.** For each policy move, write a one-sentence caveat naming the generalisation risk — what the paper's limits are, what domain difference your team has from the paper's setting, and what would make you revisit the move. The caveat is not a weakness of the memo; it is what makes the memo defensible when challenged.
6. **Add the re-check date.** Default: 6 months. A policy move you have not re-checked in a year is a policy move you are no longer auditing. The re-check date for each move can be the same (one consolidated review) or staggered.
7. **Write the one-paragraph scope and non-scope statement.** Two sentences on what the memo covers; two sentences on what it does not — the memo is not a formal performance process, is not a mandate against AI use, is not a universal prescription for every task-type.
8. **Co-review with one senior IC on your team and one peer manager.** 30 minutes each. Senior IC: *"which policy move most directly affects your work, and is the rationale accurate?"* Peer manager: *"is any of this overreaching or under-justified?"* Record the feedback in the memo's change-log.
9. **File and share.** The memo lives in the team's documentation space (not in a private manager folder). The senior ICs can reference it; the junior ICs can read it; the HR partner has a link. When you run the Chapter 5 carve-outs or the Chapter 7 prompts, the memo is what you point at for the "why."

## Starter guidance

- **Three findings, three policies, three caveats, three rationales.** Not more; not fewer. Expansion makes the memo less persuasive, not more.
- **Cite responsibly.** Follow the Chapter 2, 3, and 4 "common mistakes when citing" lists carefully. The memo is only as credible as its citations are honest.
- **Avoid the "best practices" register.** If a sentence could survive without the citation, you are not citing; you are hand-waving. Each policy move should be inarguable *because* of the specific paper, not despite it.
- **Make the policy moves *small enough to run next quarter*.** A move that requires a six-month roll-out plan is not a mod-107 policy move; it is a programme. Pick the smallest move that would create signal.
- **The caveats are features, not weaknesses.** A reader who disagrees with a policy move will usually land on the caveat first. A pre-written caveat makes the disagreement tractable instead of escalatory.
- **Do not cite papers you have not read.** A manager who cites Brynjolfsson from a secondary summary will be caught the first time a senior IC asks *"what was the sample size?"* Read the abstract, the methodology summary, and the discussion. A manager-fluency read is realistic in 30 minutes; a from-summary read is not read.
- **Draft in prose, not bullets.** The memo is readable by a human, not a document-generator. Prose makes the thinking visible.

## Acceptance criteria

The memo is complete when:

- Three policy moves, one per research anchor, each with a one-sentence rationale that cites the paper (author, year, venue, specific finding).
- Three caveats, one per move, naming a generalisation risk or a domain-specific limit.
- A re-check date per move (or one consolidated date).
- A one-paragraph scope-and-non-scope statement that names what the memo does not do (not a PIP, not an AI mandate, not a universal prescription).
- A change-log entry naming the senior-IC co-reviewer, the peer-manager co-reviewer, and the corrections produced.
- A header: title, team name, author (you), date, version `v0.1`.
- Length: two to three printed pages. Over four pages is over-written; under one page is under-justified.
- A reader who disagrees with a specific policy move can disagree with the sentence (rationale or caveat) that supports it, not with the memo as a whole.

## Stretch goals

- **Fourth paper, optional.** [Microsoft Work Trend Index](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index) data (the most recent edition) often provides *corroborating* self-report evidence consistent with the three academic findings. If a specific WTI finding corroborates one of your policy moves, add a one-sentence corroboration with the appropriate self-report-plus-vendor-authored caveats per mod-105 Chapter 6's citation discipline.
- **Measured-signal appendix.** Add a 1-page appendix naming the specific leading-indicator signals you will watch over the next 6 months to confirm or disconfirm each policy move. Link to the Chapter 7 prompts or the Chapter 5 carve-out artifacts as the measurement site.
- **Cross-team comparison.** If a peer manager is running the same exercise, trade memos. Look for findings where your teams disagree on applicability (e.g., *"Noy & Zhang applies to my writing-heavy team but not to your platform-engineering team"*). Differences surface where the papers actually do and do not generalise.
- **Second-order policy moves.** Add a 1–2 paragraph section naming *what you will not do* that the research could plausibly justify — i.e., the moves you considered and rejected, with reasons. The not-do list is often more informative than the do list.
- **Senior IC full-read.** After the co-review, ask a senior IC on your team to read the primary source of one paper (1 hour) and come back with any corrections or additions to your reading. This is both a calibration on your memo and an investment in the senior IC's judgement about the module's grounding.
