# Exercise 03 — Career Ladder Under AI Augmentation

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours.

## Objective

Produce **v0.1 of the AI-augmented career-ladder amendment** for your team: a 3–5-page artifact that layers three new rubric axes — judgement, mentorship of judgement, review craft on AI-assisted work — onto each level of your existing team ladder, writes the matching level-descriptor amendments at (at minimum) the junior / senior / staff tiers, and defines the updated promotion-packet template that reviewers use to assess the next promotion case.

This is the ladder artifact [`mod-108`](../../mod-108-hiring-performance-and-feedback-with-ai-in-the-loop/)'s performance-review and promotion-case mechanics will use as their standard. mod-107 writes the ladder; mod-108 runs the review and promotion case against it.

## Prerequisites

- [Chapter 3](../03-noy-and-zhang-and-the-differentiation-channel.md) — why the output-based promotion signal compresses under AI augmentation.
- [Chapter 6](../06-career-ladder-under-ai-augmentation.md) — the three new axes (A, B, C), the promotion-packet structure, and the composition with HR process.
- [Chapter 8](../08-ownership-boundary-with-hr.md) — the boundary with HR on comp calibration and process mechanics.
- The policy memo from [Exercise 01](exercise-01-empirical-research-into-team-policy.md) — the citation base the ladder amendment leans on.
- **Your team's or organisation's existing career ladder.** If no written ladder exists, this exercise is premature; author the baseline ladder first (through HR or mod-108) and run this exercise against it.
- Optional: a recent promotion case the team ran, as a worked-example to test the amendment against.

## Problem statement

The existing career ladder was written before generative AI was a material factor in IC output. Its rubric — impact, scope, craft, leadership — assumes that *output-shaped signal* (volume, complexity, polish) differentiates between level-N and level-N+1 work. Noy & Zhang's (Science 2023) finding on output-dispersion compression says that assumption is partly wrong now: output-shaped signal compresses, especially at junior tiers. If the ladder is not updated, three failure modes follow (Chapter 6): junior ICs get promoted on throughput before their judgement warrants it, senior ICs look flat because their leverage has moved into mentorship and review that the rubric does not weight, and staff ICs' work on the team's AI-use decisions is invisible to the committee.

The amendment is explicit. Its purpose is to make visible the parts of IC work that AI does not do for them — judgement, mentorship of judgement, review craft on AI-assisted work — and to make those parts weight-bearing in promotion decisions. The amendment is *additive*: nothing in the existing ladder is removed. The existing descriptors are extended with named AI-augmentation bullets, the rubric gains three axes, and the promotion-packet template gains two sections.

## Steps

1. **Pull the existing level-descriptor paragraphs for (at minimum) junior, senior, and staff tiers.** If your organisation has a company-wide ladder and your team operates within it, start with that ladder's descriptors. If your team has a team-scope appendix, start with that. Paste the current paragraphs into the amendment document as the baseline; your work is to layer on, not to rewrite.
2. **Draft the three axis bullets per level.** For each of the three tiers (junior, senior, staff), write three bullets — one per axis — that are:
   - Grounded in Chapter 6's Axes A, B, C descriptions.
   - Scaled appropriately: junior = recognise, senior = routinely perform, staff = set the team's direction on.
   - Specific enough to be observable: *"catches an AI-wrong decision in review at least once per quarter"* is observable; *"has strong judgement"* is not.
   - Tied to real artifacts the ladder-reader can point at — the Chapter 5 carve-out artifacts, the Chapter 7 1:1 content, the retro content, the mod-103 review standard, and the mod-106 routing matrix.
3. **Write the updated promotion-packet template.** Author the specific template a hiring manager or an IC uses to write a promotion case. Sections:
   - **Impact summary** — unchanged structure, but the examples emphasise *decisions* over *throughput*.
   - **Section 2a: Representative artifacts with judgement annotation.** For each of three representative artifacts, the IC annotates in two sentences *"what I decided the AI could draft, and what I decided to write myself."* Reviewers evaluate the decision.
   - **Section 2b: Three narratable judgement moves.** The IC describes three specific moves — a review catch, a push-back on a stakeholder plan, a redirect of a routing decision — that demonstrate judgement was active.
   - **Section 3: Mentorship of judgement — not generic mentorship.** Named ICs whose judgement capacity grew under the IC's pairing (Chapter 5 Carve-out 1 produces the evidence).
   - **Section 4: Peer feedback.** Standard quotes plus one specific added prompt: *"name a decision this IC made that you would not have expected from someone at the prior level."*
4. **Walk a recent promotion case against the amended template.** Pick a promotion the team ran in the last 12 months (ideally one that was a clear "yes" and one that was a close call). Walk each through the amended template — does the case still read as a yes? Does the close call become clearer? If the amendment produces a different outcome on a case you believe was decided correctly, something is off; iterate.
5. **Write the single-page cover document.** Two paragraphs: (a) what changed and why, citing the Exercise 01 memo for the research grounding; (b) what did *not* change — levels, compensation bands, HR process mechanics, legal / employment-law exposure. Explicit scope sharpens the artifact.
6. **Co-review with (a) a senior IC on your team, (b) a peer manager who has promoted recently, and (c) your HR partner.** Thirty minutes each.
   - Senior IC: *"does your work at the senior tier feel accurately described by the three axes? What is missing?"*
   - Peer manager: *"is any of this uncalibrated against the broader organisational ladder?"*
   - HR partner: *"does this amendment conflict with any process requirements? Where does it compose cleanly with the standard review template?"*
   - Record the feedback and corrections in the amendment's change-log.
7. **Decide the rollout.** Default: the amendment takes effect for promotion cases authored after a specific date 60–90 days from now. ICs in a cycle already authored run against the prior standard. Announce the rollout in a team meeting; link the amendment, link the Exercise 01 memo.

## Starter guidance

- **The amendment is additive, not a rewrite.** If you find yourself deleting a chunk of the existing ladder, you have overstepped. The existing descriptors are kept in full; the amendment lives alongside.
- **Three axes, not five.** Expansion dilutes signal. If the amendment grows to five or six axes, the signal the committee reads at promotion time becomes noise.
- **Observable, not aspirational.** Each axis bullet should name something a reviewer could point at and say *"this is what I mean."* Aspirational phrasing (*"brings a growth mindset to AI collaboration"*) is a red flag; rewrite.
- **Mentor outcomes, not activities.** Axis B rewards *mentorship that moved a junior IC's capacity*, not *mentorship hours*. The distinction protects against performative mentorship — which has always been a risk and is more of a risk now.
- **The staff axis is aspirational by design.** Not every senior IC will clear staff-tier Axis C. That is correct: the ladder is meant to differentiate, and Axis C differentiates at staff.
- **Do not change the junior tier much.** The lift at the junior tier is the most compressed (Brynjolfsson, Noy & Zhang both point at this); overstating what junior ICs can be expected to do on judgement is unkind and misdiagnosed.
- **Peer-review before launch.** The ladder is more legitimate if the senior ICs saw it being drafted. The co-review step is where the ladder earns its credibility.
- **Keep the promotion-packet template short.** Sections 2a, 2b, 3, 4 are the four load-bearing additions. Adding a Section 5, 6, 7 reduces reviewer compliance.
- **Avoid "AI-native" or "AI-first" phrasing in the amendment.** The phrases are marketing; they do not describe behaviour. Use the specific research-grounded language from Chapter 6 (centaur / cyborg / uncritical consumer; judgement under augmentation; etc.).

## Acceptance criteria

The amendment is complete when:

- The existing level-descriptor paragraphs for junior, senior, and staff tiers are preserved, with a three-bullet AI-augmentation amendment per tier.
- Each bullet is specifically phrased and observable — not aspirational register.
- The updated promotion-packet template has the four amended sections (2a, 2b, 3, 4) with clear instructions and an example per section (one example may be synthesised from Chapter 6's worked case).
- A one-page cover document names what changed, why (citing Exercise 01), and what did not change.
- A walked example on a recent promotion case shows the amended template produces a sensible outcome.
- A change-log entry naming the senior-IC, peer-manager, and HR-partner reviewers and the corrections produced.
- A rollout date and a brief communication plan.
- Length: 3–5 printed pages for the amendment; one page for the cover. Longer than six pages total is over-written.
- An IC up for promotion in the next cycle could read the amendment and know exactly what their packet needs to include.

## Stretch goals

- **A worked example per tier.** Author a short (2–3 paragraphs each) synthesised example of a promotion packet for junior-to-senior and senior-to-staff transitions, under the amended template. The examples make the amendment concrete for reviewers.
- **A rubric scoring note for calibration meetings.** A one-paragraph note the manager brings to calibration meetings naming how the three axes will be weighted when comparing candidates across teams. This prepares you for the *"isn't this subjective?"* challenge.
- **A junior-tier IDP tie-in.** If Exercise 05 (IDP template refresh) is already drafted, show which IDP milestones align with which axis bullet at the junior tier. The IDP → ladder connection is the growth-planning pipeline for junior ICs and should be explicit.
- **Second-reviewer round with HR.** After the first co-review and rollout, run a second HR check at the 6-month mark — specifically, after at least one promotion case has been authored under the amended template. The second check is where most calibration issues get caught.
- **A "what reviewers often get wrong" appendix.** A half-page naming the most common misreads of the amended template (e.g., treating Axis B as volume-of-mentorship; treating Section 2b as a nice-to-have; collapsing Axes A and C into "AI skill"). Reviewer training is lightweight if the appendix is good.
- **Compose with the IC-development-plan template (Exercise 05).** Walk one IC's development plan through the amended ladder for a specific next-level target. The exercise surfaces the growth-moves the IC needs to narrate in the next performance cycle.
