# Lab 02 — 1:1 Prompt-Bank Audit and Rewrite

**Duration:** 45–60 minutes.
**Chapters exercised:** Chapter 7 (the four categories of prompts; the specificity / non-judgemental-tone / rotation / follow-through principles; the Edmondson psychological-safety construct; the "common failure modes with the instrumentation" list), with side-checks against Chapter 4 (what the deference channel the Category-1 prompts probe for actually looks like), Chapter 1 (the lagged-signal argument), and Chapter 8 (why the prompts are not evidence for a formal process).
**Complements, does not duplicate:** Exercise 04 asks you to author **your team's** working set of 1:1 and retro prompts, with its own rotation guide and Westrum team-level check. This lab hands you a fixed, deliberately mixed draft prompt bank from a peer manager — so you can calibrate your *reading* of what makes a prompt work (and what makes it fail) before you spend authoring hours on your own bank.

## Objective

Read a curated sample: twelve prompts a peer manager has drafted for their 1:1s, split across the four Chapter 7 categories, deliberately mixed between strong, weak, and category-mislabelled. Score each prompt against Chapter 7's **four-signal rubric** (category fit, specificity, non-judgemental tone, follow-through possibility) and name any Chapter 7 "common failure mode" it exhibits. Then rewrite the three weakest prompts to the Chapter 7 shape. Target: **you match at least nine of the twelve scoring verdicts** with the correct failure-mode naming, and your three rewrites are usable in a 1:1 next week.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 7 read end-to-end (the four categories; the Edmondson and Westrum groundings; the "what makes the prompts work" principles; the common failure modes).
- Chapter 4 skimmed (so you recognise what the deference channel the Category-1 prompts are meant to surface actually looks like in an IC's answer).
- Chapter 1 skimmed (the lagged-signal argument — so you can defend why the prompts replace the engagement survey, not augment it).
- Chapter 8 skimmed (the explicit boundary against repurposing the prompts as performance-process evidence).
- A blank file for your scoring and rewrites.
- A timer.

## Steps

### Step 1 — Set up your working file (3 min)

Create a working file `lab-02-prompt-audit.md`. Add this header:

```
# Lab 02 — Prompt bank audit (Observability, peer manager v0.1)

Chapter 7 four-signal rubric:
  CF  Category fit    — does the prompt actually address the channel
                        the category is meant to surface?
  S   Specificity     — specific-moment narrative prompt, not a
                        feel-about-AI vague prompt
  T   Tone            — non-judgemental / curious, not interrogative
                        or audit-shaped
  FU  Follow-through  — does the prompt produce an answer the manager
                        can return to next week?

Chapter 7 common failure modes with the instrumentation:
  F-Audit     Turning the 1:1 into an atrophy audit
  F-Numeric   Numeric/rating fields that push toward survey or
              performance-management use
  F-Blame     Asking the IC to name another team member (surveillance)
  F-YesNo     Yes/no framing that forecloses the narrative
  F-Vague     "How do you feel about X" prompts that invite nothing
  F-SafetyAs  Treating low psych-safety as the IC's problem
              (asking louder instead of repairing the safety base)

Chapter 7 "what makes the prompts work":
  W1  Specificity ("walk me through a time ...")
  W2  Non-judgemental tone
  W3  Rotation across categories, not within-week repetition
  W4  Follow-through in the next 1:1 ("last week you mentioned X...")

For each prompt record: (a) category fit Y/N (if N, which category
would actually fit); (b) four-signal score CF/S/T/FU as pass/fail;
(c) failure-mode tags if any; (d) one-line verdict (keep / rewrite /
drop).
```

### Step 2 — Score Category 1 (P1, P2, P3) (6–8 min)

- Which prompt is Chapter 7's exact exemplar — specifically phrased, invites narrative, surfaces the signal you want?
- Which prompt violates the specificity principle so hard that the answer will be noise?
- Which prompt is the "turning the 1:1 into an audit" failure mode in two-sentence form?

### Step 3 — Score Category 2 (P4, P5, P6) (6–8 min)

Repeat for P4, P5, P6.

- Which prompt is the Chapter 7 exact exemplar (hint: the chapter uses a version of it in its Category 2 list, with a clear reference to the routing matrix)?
- Which prompt is the "numeric rating" failure mode — a rating-field attempt that pushes the instrument toward survey / performance-management use (same shape as the Chapter 5 Carve-out 2 failure "ICs feel the AI-off slot is a test")?
- Which prompt is category-correct and well-phrased but Category-2-exemplar rather than Category-1 — meaning its signal is about *dependency*, not *in-the-moment deference*?

### Step 4 — Score Category 3 (P7, P8, P9) (6–8 min)

Repeat for P7, P8, P9.

- Which prompt is Chapter 7's exact exemplar on the attention-shift risk and the post-automation-attention question?
- Which prompt is the yes/no framing failure mode (the IC's honest answer is almost always "no" even when it's yes, because the frame forecloses the narrative)?
- Which prompt is well-phrased in intent but potentially *too interrogative* in affect — what's the fix without changing the content?

Repeat for P10, P11, P12.

- Which prompt is the surveillance-repurposing failure mode — asking the IC to name *another* IC's behaviour rather than their own? (Chapter 7 is explicit about why this breaks psychological safety.)
- Which prompt is the "aggregated numeric trust rating" failure mode — the same anti-pattern as the engagement survey this instrument is meant to replace?

### Step 6 — Compare against the answer key (8 min)

Read the key below only after you have scored all twelve prompts.

#### Answer key — prompt-by-prompt verdicts

**P1 — *"How do you feel about AI?"***

- **Verdict: drop.** Fails CF (addresses nothing specifically); fails S (vague); fails FU (no material to return to next week). Failure-mode tag: **F-Vague**. Chapter 7 is explicit: *"Specificity. 'Walk me through a time' is a stronger prompt than 'how do you feel about AI.' The specificity invites a narrative; the narrative surfaces the behaviour."* This is a Category-0 prompt — a prompt about the tool in the abstract, not about the IC's work.

**P2 — *"Walk me through a time this week you accepted an AI suggestion without changing it. What made it right to accept?"***

- **Verdict: keep as the Category-1 exemplar.** Passes CF, S, T, FU. This is Chapter 7's exact phrasing for the Category-1 deference prompt; the IC's answer will either ground the acceptance in judgement ("it was a mechanical refactor, I've done hundreds, the diff looked right") or surface deference ("it looked plausible and I was running short on time") — and either way the answer is diagnostic and returnable next week.

**P3 — *"Are you over-relying on AI? Be honest."***

- **Verdict: drop.** Fails T hard (reads as audit — "be honest" pre-signals the asker's disbelief); fails CF (the question frames the IC as the problem); fails FU (the honest answer is almost always "no," and if the IC says "yes" the manager has no specific material to return to). Failure-mode tags: **F-Audit, F-SafetyAs**. Chapter 7: *"The deference prompts can read as interrogative if the manager's affect is sharp. The right tone is curious, not audit-like. If the IC feels examined, the next week's prompt gets a performative answer."* The "be honest" suffix is the specific audit tell.

**P4 — *"On the three outside-frontier task-types in our routing matrix, do you feel you could do them without the AI if it were unavailable tomorrow? What specifically would be harder?"***

- **Verdict: keep.** Passes CF (grounds the question in named task-types from the mod-106 routing matrix), S (specific — not "do you depend on AI," but "on these three task-types, how much"), T (curious framing — "what specifically would be harder" invites narrative, not confession), FU (the answer sets up the Carve-out 2 AI-off artifact conversation for the quarter). This is Chapter 7's Category-2-plus-routing-matrix pattern, and it is strong.

**P5 — *"Rate your AI dependency on a 1–5 scale: 1 = independent, 5 = fully dependent."***

- **Verdict: drop.** Fails S (a scalar rating is less diagnostic than any specific-moment prompt); fails T (the word "dependency" with a numeric rating reads as a diagnostic instrument — performance-management shape); fails FU (a "3" produces no material for next week's follow-up). Failure-mode tag: **F-Numeric**. The rating is the same anti-pattern Chapter 5 flags for the Carve-out 2 artifact ("ICs feel tested") and Chapter 7 Exercise 04 names explicitly: numeric ratings in 1:1 prompts push the instrument toward survey / performance-management use and corrupt the signal. Chapter 7 is also clear that the burnout category uses a numeric framing at most once as a rotation prompt and even then names the number as "less interesting than the direction of change week-over-week" — a scalar like this, framed as dependency, is worse.

**P6 — *"What's one thing you're doing with AI today that six months ago you were doing yourself — and do you think you could still do it?"***

- **Verdict: keep as the Category-2 exemplar.** Passes CF, S, T, FU. Chapter 7 gives this exact prompt in its Category-2 list. The answer tells the manager whether the IC has an internal model of what they have delegated — which is the growth-path / dependency signal.

**P7 — *"What's the thing you're doing more of, now that AI handles the routine slice? Does it feel like better work or just different work?"***

- **Verdict: keep as the Category-3 exemplar.** Passes CF, S, T, FU. Chapter 7 gives this exact prompt. The answer reveals whether the post-automation attention is going somewhere productive — which is the specific attention-shift burnout signal (and the mod-106 Chapter 7 anti-destination of "drift back into the automated work").

**P8 — *"Are you burning out? Yes or no."***

- **Verdict: drop.** Fails S (binary, no narrative); fails T (clinical / audit-shaped); fails FU (the honest answer is almost always "no" and the dishonest answer produces nothing to return to). Failure-mode tag: **F-YesNo**. Even if the IC says "yes," the manager has no handhold for what to do next without the surrounding narrative. The under-specified Chapter 7 burnout prompt — *"On a scale where 1 is 'I feel I'm coasting' and 5 is 'I feel I'm drowning' — where were you this week?"* — is rotated in sparingly and is better than a binary; even that one the chapter describes as "direct, under-specified, cheap" with the number "less interesting than the direction of change week-over-week."

**P9 — *"If I were watching you work, how much of your time would be spent reviewing AI output? Is that a cognitively expensive share?"***

- **Verdict: keep with a tone tweak.** Passes CF (addresses the attention-shift signal), S (specific — "how much of your time," "cognitively expensive share"), FU (returnable), but the T is borderline — *"if I were watching you work"* can read as surveillant to an IC in a low-safety relationship. The tone fix is to drop the opening clause: *"How much of your time this week went into reviewing AI output rather than producing it? Was that an expensive share, cognitively?"* — same signal, less surveillant framing.

**P10 — *"Who on the team is over-relying on AI?"***

- **Verdict: drop (hard).** Fails CF, T, FU across the board. Failure-mode tag: **F-Blame, F-Audit** at team scope. Chapter 7 is explicit that the prompts are *"diagnostic and growth-oriented; repurposing them as evidence for a performance process breaks psychological safety for the IC and, if they realise the shift, for the entire team."* Asking the IC to name *another* IC's behaviour is worse — it conscripts the IC into a surveillance role and is the reverse of Edmondson's psychological-safety construct. Even if the manager is sincerely trying to surface a pattern, this phrasing produces false positives (politics), false negatives (loyalty), and corrupts the 1:1 relationship for both the asked and the named. Chapter 7's retro-level signal on who is over-relying comes from *aggregate* retro content (Westrum lens on how the team handles specific AI-wrong cases) and from *each IC's own* 1:1 content — never from asking one IC to name another.

**P11 — *"Name one case this sprint where the AI suggested something and we made the judgement call to go another way. What made the judgement call work?"***

- **Verdict: keep as the Category-4 exemplar.** Passes CF, S, T, FU. Chapter 7 gives this prompt verbatim in its Category-4 list. Its purpose is to build the team's narrative muscle around judgement moves — the artifacts the Chapter 6 ladder will reward. It is positive-signal-oriented (not blame-framed), it is specific (named sprint), and it feeds the Chapter 5 Carve-out 1 rotation planning for the next quarter.

**P12 — *"On a scale of 1–10, how much is the team trusting AI output?"***

- **Verdict: drop.** Fails S (scalar, team-level); fails CF (the question is a survey-shaped engagement-pulse question, not a retro prompt); fails FU (an aggregate "7" produces no mechanism for next retro). Failure-mode tag: **F-Numeric**. This is the specific anti-pattern Chapter 7 names when it says the 1:1 and retro *replace* the engagement survey's role in detecting the atrophy signal — the aggregated numeric rating is the engagement-survey anti-pattern the whole chapter is designed to displace. The right Category-4 prompt asks for *specifics* (a named case the team accepted and later wished it hadn't; a named case the team pushed back and the push-back worked); the number is what the survey already provides and is what the retro is explicitly instrumented against.

Count hits. **Nine of twelve verdicts matching, with the correct failure-mode tag on each dropped prompt, is the pass bar.** Seven or fewer means a re-read of Chapter 7's "The prompts, by category" and "What makes the prompts work" sections before starting Exercise 04.

### Step 7 — Rewrite the three worst prompts in Chapter 7 shape (10–12 min)

Pick three of the prompts you marked "drop" and rewrite each into a Chapter 7-shaped Category-correct prompt. The rewrite should:

- Match the right category (rewrite a Category-1 drop into a Category-1 prompt, etc.).
- Satisfy the four-signal rubric: specific moment, curious tone, returnable next week.
- Not re-introduce the failure mode you flagged (no numeric rating; no yes/no; no "who on the team"; no "be honest").
- Read naturally — *"walk me through a time..."* not *"describe an instance in which..."*

Example shape (do not copy verbatim):

```
Original P3 (drop, F-Audit, F-SafetyAs):
  "Are you over-relying on AI? Be honest."

Rewrite (Category 1, deference):
  "Walk me through a time this week you rejected or modified an AI
   suggestion. What tipped you off?"

Rationale: moves from a confession-framed audit question to a specific-
  moment narrative prompt. If the IC can't name a rejection over a
  month of 1:1s (Chapter 7's exact diagnostic criterion), that is the
  deference-channel signal — surfaced without framing the IC as the
  problem, which keeps the safety base intact for the next 1:1.
  Pairs with Carve-out 2 scheduling at quarter boundary.

Original P10 (drop, F-Blame, F-Audit at team scope):
  "Who on the team is over-relying on AI?"

Rewrite (Category 4, retro):
  "Name one case this sprint where we accepted an AI output and
   later wished we hadn't. What was missing from the review?"

Rationale: moves from naming a person to naming a review-mechanism gap
  (per Chapter 7's blameless framing, mod-109 posture). Produces
  actionable material for the next Carve-out 3 scope decision; does
  not conscript any IC into a surveillance role. Chapter 7 gives
  this as a Category-4 exemplar.

[... one more rewrite in the same shape ...]
```

If any rewrite still carries a numeric rating, a yes/no, a "be honest," or a "who on the team" shape, it has drifted; revise.

### Step 8 — Spot-check the rotation and tone (5 min)

A rotation is a background property of the bank, not a property of individual prompts, but the lab tests one calibration on it: pick two kept prompts from different categories and sketch, in one sentence each, *when* in a four-week rotation you would ask them of **the same IC**. Chapter 7's default is one prompt from a rotating category per weekly 1:1, so each IC sees one Category-1, one Category-2, and one Category-3 prompt per month plus Category-4 at retros on a quarterly cadence.

If your sketch has two Category-1 prompts in consecutive weeks, re-read Chapter 7's "Rotation" principle before publishing your own bank.
