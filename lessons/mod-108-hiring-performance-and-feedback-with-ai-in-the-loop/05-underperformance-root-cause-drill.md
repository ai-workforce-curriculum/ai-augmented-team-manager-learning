# Chapter 5 — Underperformance Root-Cause Framing: Human, Workflow, or Tooling?

> *An IC on your team is shipping below the bar. Under AI augmentation there are three plausible root causes and they take three very different interventions. Diagnose the wrong one — treat a tooling gap as a human-skill gap, or vice versa — and the intervention makes the signal worse. This chapter is the drill the manager runs before deciding what to do about the shortfall, and before the PIP conversation with HR.*

## Motivation

Before AI augmentation, the common framing of underperformance ran along two axes: a skill gap (the IC does not yet know how to do the work) or a motivation / fit gap (the IC can do the work but something else is in the way). Those two axes still apply. Under AI augmentation a third axis appears that is genuinely distinct and genuinely common: the IC is caught in a workflow or tooling gap that the mod-105 enablement programme or the mod-106 routing matrix is supposed to catch but has not.

The drill in this chapter prevents a common and costly failure mode: the manager observes that an IC's output is below the team baseline, runs the pre-AI diagnostic (skill or motivation?), concludes it is a skill or motivation gap, opens a performance conversation with the IC and HR — and then surfaces in the first PIP check-in that the IC was being routed outside-frontier work the AI could not help with, had no access to the team's enablement bootcamp from mod-105, and was isolated from the paired-review rotation from mod-107. The intervention needed was a workflow-and-tooling fix, not a performance process. The IC's trust, the team's trust, and the manager's credibility are all damaged.

Exercise 04 produces a practised drill — a short, repeatable set of diagnostic questions the manager runs *before* deciding what the root cause is, and before any coordinated conversation with HR. The output is a specific diagnosis and a specific intervention plan tied to the right module ([`mod-105`](../mod-105-team-ai-enablement-programs/), [`mod-106`](../mod-106-workflow-redesign-and-agent-delegation/), or Chapter 4's 1:1 flow and [`mod-107` Chapter 8](../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/08-ownership-boundary-with-hr.md)'s HR partnership).

## Core concepts

### The three root causes, named plainly

- **Human.** The IC has a skill gap, a judgement gap, a motivation issue, a fit question, or a life-circumstance issue affecting their work. The intervention lives in the manager's 1:1 and growth work (Chapter 4), the mod-107 carve-outs, and — if the pattern does not shift — in the Handoff 1 conversation with HR ([`mod-107` Chapter 8](../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/08-ownership-boundary-with-hr.md)).
- **Workflow.** The IC is being routed work the routing matrix should have routed elsewhere (to a different IC, to a paired-review, to the agent-takes-first-cut path), or the work's human-in-the-loop checkpoints are wrong for the task shape, or the SDLC the team is running does not fit the IC's work. The intervention lives in mod-106 — the routing matrix update, the delegation ladder refinement, the HITL-checkpoint review.
- **Tooling.** The IC is missing access to the AI tools the rest of the team uses, is on an older model or a slower seat, did not complete the mod-105 enablement bootcamp, or the tool is genuinely poor on the task shape the IC is working on. The intervention lives in mod-105 (enablement, bootcamp, communities of practice) or mod-111 (tool / seat / access).

The three causes can compose. An IC might have a modest skill gap *and* be routed outside-frontier work *and* be on an older seat than the team's senior ICs. Treating any one axis in isolation misses the composition; the drill is explicit about surfacing all three.

### The drill, as a short checklist

The drill runs in about an hour of the manager's time, before any decision is made. Six questions:

1. **Shape-of-work check.** Is the work the IC is doing in-frontier for the AI the team uses, or outside? If outside, is the IC getting the paired-review / Human-takes-first-cut routing (mod-106) that outside-frontier work requires?
2. **Tool-access check.** Does the IC have the same seat, model, and tool access as the senior ICs on the team? When did their access change last? Did they complete the mod-105 bootcamp? Are they participating in the community-of-practice?
3. **Enablement check.** Did the IC go through the Day 0 / 30 / 60 / 90 ramp (mod-105)? Did they skip or defer any specific piece? Did the team deliver the piece? (It is common for a bootcamp piece to be skipped for a hire during a busy quarter and the IC never recovers the missing scaffolding.)
4. **Pairing check.** Who on the team has paired with the IC in the last two quarters? If nobody has, the IC has been isolated from the mod-107 paired-review rotation, which is one of the primary mechanisms by which the team builds the judgement muscle. Isolation is a workflow failure, not an IC failure.
5. **Workflow-friction check.** When the IC describes their work in a 1:1 (the time-and-attention question from Chapter 4), what are the specific friction points? Is the IC spending unexpected time on agent-output correction (tooling signal)? Is the IC blocked waiting for review that should be automated (workflow signal)? Is the IC confused about expectations (growth signal)?
6. **Human signal check.** Only after the first five: in 1:1s, has the IC described patterns that are specifically human — a personal situation, a motivation problem, a fit question, a skill gap they are unable to close despite effort? Does the pattern show up specifically on *judgement* (Axis A from the ladder) rather than on throughput?

The drill is explicit in its ordering. The first five questions surface workflow and tooling root causes. Only after those are excluded (or, if one is present, addressed with the right mod-105 / mod-106 / mod-111 intervention) does the drill turn to the human-skill question. The ordering prevents the failure mode of treating a routing problem as a performance problem.

### What the drill is not

- **Not a scorecard.** The drill is not six Yes / No answers producing a score. Each answer is a specific observation the manager writes down so the composition of causes is visible.
- **Not a shield against the hard conversation.** If the drill surfaces that the IC has a genuine human-side pattern — skill gap, motivation, fit — the growth conversation still happens, the mod-107 carve-outs still run, and the Handoff 1 conversation with HR is still opened if the pattern does not shift. The drill prevents *premature* escalation; it does not prevent escalation when warranted.
- **Not a checklist to run once.** A pattern that was workflow-rooted in Q1 can become human-rooted in Q3 if nothing changes in the IC's trajectory. The drill is re-run when the signal re-surfaces, not run once as a boundary ritual.

### The intervention that follows each root cause

**Human root cause.** Run the Chapter 4 1:1 flow with the specific question that targets the gap. Add the mod-107 carve-outs (Carve-out 2 — AI-off artifact — is especially useful when the gap is a judgement-muscle gap). Draft the half-page written feedback artifact that names the pattern specifically and what the IC should produce next quarter. If after two quarters of this intervention the pattern has not shifted, run the Handoff 1 conversation with HR per [`mod-107` Chapter 8](../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/08-ownership-boundary-with-hr.md).

**Workflow root cause.** Update the mod-106 routing matrix for the specific task shape. If the paired-review rotation is missing for an IC, add it. If the HITL checkpoints are wrong, adjust them. The intervention is operational and completes within a cycle or two. The IC's performance signal typically recovers within one quarter of the routing correction.

**Tooling root cause.** Check tool access and model tier; close the gap via mod-105 enablement work (bootcamp retake, pairing with a senior IC on a specific tool) and mod-111 (budget / seat / vendor). If the tool is genuinely poor on the task shape and no available tool handles it, update the routing matrix to route that task shape to a different workflow (back to workflow root cause — the two often compose).

**Composed root cause.** All three causes are addressed in sequence: tooling first (quick to fix, unblocks the IC), workflow second (medium-term fix, unblocks the IC's learning), human-growth third (long-term, requires carve-outs and feedback). The IC is given the fastest possible path to shipping at the level the team expects; the manager retains the growth conversation but does not open a formal process while the workflow and tooling gaps are being closed.

### The PIP contract with HR, when it is warranted

A performance improvement plan is a formal document owned by HR / employee-relations. The manager's side of the work, before the PIP is written, is:

- **Two quarters of documented growth conversation.** The quarterly written feedback artifacts from Chapter 4 provide this. One quarter of signal is noise; two is a pattern.
- **A documented check against the drill in this chapter.** The manager can show that workflow and tooling root causes were considered and either excluded or addressed. The IC was given the mod-105 / mod-106 / mod-111 interventions where applicable.
- **The Handoff 1 conversation with HR, framed as an open question.** The manager brings the two quarters of documentation and the drill output to HR and asks *"is this the kind of pattern that warrants a formal process, or should I continue to work on it in 1:1s?"* The question is open; the manager does not pre-decide. HR assesses with the manager.
- **If HR and the manager jointly decide to open a PIP, the manager's role shifts** per [`mod-107` Chapter 8](../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/08-ownership-boundary-with-hr.md) Handoff 3. 1:1 documentation protocol changes, HR owns the plan, the manager coordinates and participates per the organisation's process. The content of the PIP — specific improvement criteria, timeline, review cadence — is drafted jointly; HR approves and holds the process.

The specific content of a PIP is organisation-dependent and is not the manager's unilateral drafting. The manager's contribution is the evidence base and the specific behaviours that the PIP will measure against. Under AI augmentation the behaviours are typically ladder-axis-aligned (e.g., *"produces at least one narratable judgement move per month, documented in 1:1 notes, by end of quarter"*) rather than throughput-aligned.

### What *not* to do

- **Do not run the drill in front of the IC.** The drill is the manager's instrument. Running it as an interrogation of the IC ("let me ask you six questions about why you're underperforming") breaks the growth frame. The drill informs the manager's assessment; the subsequent 1:1 conversation uses the results without framing itself as the drill.
- **Do not skip the first five questions because the human cause "seems obvious."** The failure mode this chapter prevents is specifically the manager who skips the workflow and tooling questions because their intuition says it is the IC. Intuition under AI augmentation is miscalibrated; the first five questions re-calibrate.
- **Do not open an informal PIP.** As [`mod-107` Chapter 8](../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/08-ownership-boundary-with-hr.md) is explicit about: scheduling weekly check-ins with explicit improvement milestones is a PIP whether it is called one or not. If the formal process is warranted, open it; if not, the Chapter 4 1:1 flow is the instrument.
- **Do not cite research about AI deference as evidence against a specific IC.** The research (Dell'Acqua, Noy & Zhang, Brynjolfsson) is about populations. It is justification for the drill and for the ladder; it is not evidence that *this specific IC* is deferring. For a specific IC, the evidence is their specific PRs, their specific 1:1 narration, and their specific feedback-artifact history.
- **Do not let a tooling gap persist once identified.** If the drill surfaces that the IC is on an older seat or missed the bootcamp, close the gap in the week it surfaces. A tooling gap left open for another quarter while the IC is "on notice" is a specifically unfair position to put the IC in.

### Common failure modes in running the drill

- **Running the drill once in year one and never again.** Routing, tooling, and team composition change; the drill is re-run when the signal re-surfaces.
- **Running the drill but not writing the output down.** Memory of the drill does not survive to the Handoff 1 conversation with HR. Write the output down.
- **Conflating "the IC is slower than the team baseline" with underperformance.** The team baseline moved under AI augmentation. If the IC is slower but is making narratable judgement moves and growing on Axis A/B/C, they may be performing well on the dimensions that matter. The drill is about *underperformance*, not about *slower throughput*.
- **Using the drill as a management-theatre ritual.** The drill is substantive; running it as a box-check without engaging with the specific IC's work does not produce useful signal and corrodes the manager's seriousness about the IC's development.
- **Running the drill on multiple ICs in parallel.** The drill is per-IC. Running it across the team in one batch produces generic results; the specificity is the signal.

## Example: a drill that caught a workflow problem

Composite example; calibration, not a case study.

A manager observes that Mid-Level IC D's throughput is 40% below the team baseline and the quarterly feedback artifact for Q2 is thin on all three ladder axes. The pre-AI intuition is "this is a performance conversation." The manager runs the drill before taking action.

1. **Shape of work.** IC D has been on three outside-frontier projects in a row — the kinds of work the routing matrix should have routed to paired-review or human-takes-first-cut. The matrix did not get updated when IC D rotated into the subteam; the routing default was "agent-takes-first-cut." Signal: workflow root cause.
2. **Tool access.** IC D is on the same seat as the team's senior ICs. Did complete the Day 0 / 30 / 60 bootcamp. Did not attend the last two community-of-practice sessions — at the manager's own direction, because IC D had been on a production-incident rotation. Signal: tooling access fine; enablement participation slightly reduced by manager decision.
3. **Enablement.** Day 90 piece — the first solo outside-frontier project with a senior pair — was skipped because the senior IC on IC D's planned pairing was on leave and the pair was not rescheduled. Signal: enablement gap.
4. **Pairing.** Nobody has paired with IC D in two quarters. Signal: isolation from the mod-107 paired-review rotation.
5. **Workflow friction.** IC D's 1:1 narration in the last four weeks has included multiple time-and-attention complaints specifically about agent-output correction and about feeling unsure when the agent's output is wrong. Signal: consistent with outside-frontier work and absence of pairing.
6. **Human signal.** After the first five, a modest residual human signal: IC D is unsure about their work, has not made a narratable judgement move in two quarters. But the signal is *composed with* the four workflow/tooling signals, not independent.

The manager's intervention sequence:

- Week 1: reroute IC D's next project to a routing-matrix-correct shape (paired-review on an outside-frontier task); tool access is fine; schedule the missed Day 90 pairing with a senior IC.
- Week 2–6: re-establish the paired-review rotation (mod-107); re-attach IC D to the community-of-practice.
- Week 8: run Chapter 4's monthly 1:1 to see if the judgement-move narration starts to surface under the new routing. (It does, modestly.)
- Week 12: quarterly written feedback artifact names the Q3 improvement explicitly and the growth moves for Q4.
- No performance conversation opened. No HR escalation. The drill correctly diagnosed a composed workflow / enablement root cause with a residual human-growth component; the interventions were operational, fast, and visible to IC D.

A version of the example where the drill had not been run: manager opens a performance conversation at the end of Q2, HR draws up a PIP for Q3, IC D goes through two months of formal process with no change in routing, exits the PIP still on outside-frontier work with no pairing, and leaves the company. The IC is lost; the team baseline did not improve; the organisation did not learn what to fix.

## Summary

- **Three root causes under AI augmentation:** human, workflow, tooling. The drill surfaces all three; treating any one in isolation is the common failure mode.
- **Six-question drill** in a strict order: shape-of-work, tool-access, enablement, pairing, workflow-friction, human-signal. The order prevents premature escalation.
- **Interventions per cause:** human → Chapter 4 1:1 flow + mod-107 carve-outs; workflow → mod-106 routing matrix + HITL checkpoints; tooling → mod-105 enablement + mod-111 tool / seat.
- **Composed root cause is common** — tooling first, workflow second, human third, in sequence.
- **PIP contracts with HR** require two quarters of documented growth work (Chapter 4), a documented drill output excluding or addressing workflow and tooling, and the open-question Handoff 1 conversation with HR per [`mod-107` Chapter 8](../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/08-ownership-boundary-with-hr.md). HR owns the formal process; the manager contributes the evidence and participates per organisation protocol.
- **What not to do:** run the drill in front of the IC, skip the first five questions, open an informal PIP, cite population research as evidence against an individual, let a tooling gap persist once identified.
- **Common failure modes:** running the drill once and never again, not writing the output down, conflating slower throughput with underperformance, management-theatre application, batch-running across the team.
- **Composes with** [`mod-105`](../mod-105-team-ai-enablement-programs/) (enablement interventions for tooling / enablement root cause), [`mod-106`](../mod-106-workflow-redesign-and-agent-delegation/) (routing-matrix updates for workflow root cause), [`mod-107`](../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/) (carve-outs for human-growth root cause; Handoff 1 boundary with HR).
