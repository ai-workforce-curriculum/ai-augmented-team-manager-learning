# Exercise 02 — Levelling Criteria Refresh

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours.

## Objective

Produce **v0.1 of your team's levelling-criteria refresh**: a 2–3-page artifact that composes with the ladder authored in [`mod-107` Chapter 6](../../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/06-career-ladder-under-ai-augmentation.md) and adds four operational pieces — a weighting rubric per transition, a one-page reviewer-reading guide, a pre-cycle conversation script, and a calibration-defence note — so the ladder actually differentiates in a live cycle.

This exercise assumes the mod-107 ladder update exists (the three axes, the level-descriptor amendments, the promotion-packet structure). If your team has not done that work yet, start there first — this exercise runs *against* the ladder, not *in place of* it.

## Prerequisites

- [Chapter 3](../03-levelling-criteria-refresh.md) — the four pieces the refresh adds, the weighting rubric structure, the three failure modes it prevents.
- [`mod-107` Chapter 6](../../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/06-career-ladder-under-ai-augmentation.md) — the ladder update this exercise composes with.
- [`mod-107` Chapter 3](../../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/03-noy-and-zhang-and-the-differentiation-channel.md) — the research grounding ([Noy & Zhang](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586)) for weighting throughput down.
- Your team's **current ladder** (team-scope or team appendix to an org ladder) with the mod-107 amendments applied.
- Two recent promotion packets from your team — ideally one clear-yes and one close-call — to walk the refresh against.

## Problem statement

The mod-107 ladder update specifies three new axes (judgement, mentorship of judgement, review craft) and amends the level descriptors. Running a real calibration cycle against that ladder surfaces four operational gaps: the axes are present but there is no guidance on *weighting* them at different transitions; reviewers read packets against the pre-AI rubric they have internalised unless given a guide; the IC's six-month runway to the packet is unused because there is no structured pre-cycle conversation; and in calibration, the manager has to argue against peer managers citing throughput numbers in real time with no prepared note.

The refresh closes those gaps. It is additive to the ladder, not a rewrite. Length should be 2–3 pages — the weighting rubric fits in a table, the reviewer-reading guide is one page by design, the pre-cycle conversation script is half a page, the calibration-defence note is one paragraph.

## Steps

1. **Write the weighting rubric.** A table with one row per transition (junior → mid, mid → senior, senior → staff, staff → principal if your team has that level) and one column per axis (Axis A judgement, Axis B mentorship of judgement, Axis C review craft, plus throughput/impact and scope/ownership). Fill in weights (low / moderate / high / very high) grounded in Chapter 3. The specific weights are suggestive, not canonical — tune them to your team's level transitions.
2. **Author the reviewer-reading guide (one page, maximum).** For each of the three new axes, write: what signal the axis is supposed to produce on a packet, what the common misread is, what a strong packet shows. Written for the reviewer's frame (what to look for), not the manager's frame (why the candidate is ready). The guide is attached to every promotion packet submitted to the committee.
3. **Write the pre-cycle conversation script.** The 1:1 the manager runs with the IC six months before a packet is due. Four prompts, in order: which axes is the IC's work currently producing signal on; where is the gap; what are the next-two-quarter growth moves; what will the packet need to show six months from now. Script is half a page; it is a conversation scaffold, not a form the IC fills in.
4. **Write the calibration-defence note.** One paragraph the manager brings to the calibration meeting naming how the three axes will be compared across candidates from different teams, with the research citation ([Noy & Zhang on dispersion compression](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586)) in hand. Write a specific example anticipating a peer manager's likely throughput-based comparison and the manager's response.
5. **Walk two recent packets against the refreshed rubric.** Pick one clear-yes and one close-call packet from the last 12 months. Score each against the refreshed rubric. For the clear-yes, confirm the refresh still produces a yes — if not, the rubric is miscalibrated. For the close-call, confirm the refresh produces a clearer answer in either direction. Record the walk-through in the artifact.
6. **Co-review with a senior IC, a peer manager, and HR.** Thirty minutes each.
   - Senior IC: *"does the weighting at your tier match how you see the work?"*
   - Peer manager: *"is any of this uncalibrated against the broader organisation's ladder?"*
   - HR: *"does the refresh compose cleanly with the organisation's promotion process? Does the reviewer-reading guide belong attached to the packet or kept separate?"*
   - Record feedback and corrections in a change-log.
7. **Rollout.** Default: the refresh takes effect for packets authored after a specific date 60–90 days from now. ICs already drafting under the prior standard complete under the prior standard. Announce in a team meeting; link the refresh and the [`mod-107` Chapter 6](../../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/06-career-ladder-under-ai-augmentation.md) ladder.

## Starter guidance

- **The refresh is additive, not a rewrite.** The ladder authored in mod-107 Chapter 6 stays; the refresh is four pieces of operational scaffolding around it. If the refresh grows into a ladder rewrite, scope has crept.
- **Write the reviewer-reading guide for the reviewer, not the manager.** The audience is a reviewer who has 30 minutes to read the packet and the guide. One page, scannable, specific.
- **The weighting rubric is not a scorecard.** Reviewers do not multiply weights by scores. The weights shape the calibration conversation ("Axis A is weighted very high at senior → staff; this packet is thin on Axis A").
- **Throughput weight is never zero** at any transition. Throughput still matters; it is weighted down at senior-and-above because [the research](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586) predicts it compresses. An IC with throughput far below baseline has a performance conversation ([Chapter 5](../05-underperformance-root-cause-drill.md)), not a promotion packet.
- **The pre-cycle conversation runs at 6 months, not 3.** Three-month runway is too short for Axis B evidence (mentorship) to materialise. The six-month runway is the window that still has effect.
- **The calibration-defence note is written down before the meeting.** Managers who try to compose the argument in the room lose to peer managers citing throughput numbers. One paragraph, specific comparison, research citation.
- **Walk the recent packets individually, not the whole set.** The per-packet walk catches rubric drift; batch walking produces averages that hide the drift.
- **Keep the artifact short.** The refresh is 2–3 pages. If it grows past five pages the reviewer will not use it, and the whole value is in reviewer use.
- **Avoid introducing a fourth AI-specific axis.** The ladder has three. Adding dilutes the signal.

## Acceptance criteria

The artifact is complete when:

- The weighting rubric table is filled in for every level transition the team runs.
- The reviewer-reading guide is one page, written for the reviewer's frame, with named common misreads per axis.
- The pre-cycle conversation script has four prompts in the specified order; half a page.
- The calibration-defence note is one paragraph; names a specific comparison scenario with research citation.
- Two recent packets have been walked against the refreshed rubric; the walks are recorded in the artifact.
- Change-log entries name the senior IC, peer manager, and HR reviewers and the corrections produced.
- A rollout date and communication plan are named.
- The total artifact is 2–3 pages (cover + table + guide + script + note + walk-through notes). More than five pages is over-written.
- An IC up for promotion in the next cycle could read the refresh (plus the underlying ladder) and know exactly what their packet needs to show.
- A promotion-committee reviewer could read the guide (plus the packet) and avoid the three failure modes Chapter 3 names.

## Stretch goals

- **A worked example per transition.** For each level transition, a short (two-paragraph) synthesised packet example that demonstrates what "strong on this axis at this transition" looks like.
- **A rater-reading quiz.** Two anonymised packet excerpts; have the reviewer identify the axis-A / B / C signal in each. The exercise surfaces which parts of the reviewer-reading guide land and which do not.
- **Compose with Exercise 03.** Map each IC's current OKRs (from Exercise 03) to the ladder axes they are producing signal on. The composition gives the manager a quarterly read on whether the growth moves from the pre-cycle conversation are actually showing up in the goals the IC is pursuing.
- **A junior-tier IDP tie-in.** If your team maintains an IDP template (composing with [`mod-107` Exercise 05](../../mod-107-skill-growth-anti-atrophy-and-career-development/exercises/exercise-05-ic-development-plan-template-refresh.md)), show which IDP milestones align with which axis bullet at the junior tier.
- **A second-reviewer round after one live cycle.** Six months after the rollout — and at least one promotion packet authored under the refresh — re-run the co-reviews specifically on "what was miscalibrated?" The second review catches most of the long-tail calibration errors.
- **A one-page comparison to the pre-AI rubric.** Short artifact: for each axis in the refresh, what the pre-AI rubric scored for in that territory. The comparison is useful for reviewers who have been promoting under the pre-AI rubric for several years.
