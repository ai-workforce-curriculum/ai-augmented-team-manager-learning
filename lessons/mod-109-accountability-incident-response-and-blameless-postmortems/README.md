# mod-109 — Accountability, AI-Incident Response, and Blameless Post-Mortems

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours.

## What this module does

Turns the **accountability contract** authored in [`mod-103` Chapter 5](../mod-103-review-standards-and-eval-commissioning/05-the-accountability-contract-ai-generates-humans-own.md) from a pinned paragraph on the team wiki into **an operational incident-response practice at team scope**. Defines the end-to-end flow when an AI-produced artifact ships wrong (who owns the response, what gets logged, what rolls back, how the team learns), adapts the blameless post-mortem discipline from [Google SRE](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/) and [John Allspaw's *Blameless Post-Mortems*](https://www.etsy.com/codeascraft/blameless-postmortems/) to AI-produced failures, triages four AI-specific incident classes (hallucination-driven harm, prompt-injection exploitation, unauthorised-tool-use, model regression) against public precedents in the [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/) and the [OECD.AI AI Incidents Monitor](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents), and names the routing contract with `ai-risk-engineer` (peer, level 25, AI Governance family) and SecOps. Closes the loop by turning every confirmed incident into a permanent regression fixture that feeds back into the mod-103 eval commissioning and the mod-106 delegation playbook.

This module is **not** an engineered red-team or alignment-eval course — that depth lives sideways at [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (level 25) and [`agentic-safety-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/agentic-safety-engineer-learning) (level 40, AI Governance family). It is **not** a SOC / DFIR course — SOC-side incident handling belongs to security operations. It is **not** a legal-opinion course — regulator engagement and disclosure belong to counsel. The manager-scope deliverable is the *team's* operating practice and the routing contracts to the specialists.

## Learning objectives

- Operate the accountability contract from [`mod-103`](../mod-103-review-standards-and-eval-commissioning/) end-to-end — when an agent ships wrong output, who owns the response, what gets logged, what rolls back, and how the team learns.
- Adapt blameless post-mortem practice ([Google SRE](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/), [Allspaw](https://www.etsy.com/codeascraft/blameless-postmortems/)) for AI-produced incidents — the team cannot blame the model but still must find the fault chain (the human reviewer, the harness, the prompt, the agent policy, the tool connection).
- Triage AI-specific incidents at team scope — hallucination-driven harm, prompt-injection exploitation, unauthorised-tool-use, model regression — using the [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/) and [OECD.AI Incidents Monitor](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents) as public precedents.
- Coordinate with [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (peer, level 25, AI Governance family) on the engineered root-cause chain; coordinate with security / SecOps for the SOC-side chain; keep team-scope communication clean and leadership-ready.
- Turn every confirmed incident into a permanent regression fixture — fold findings back into [`mod-103`](../mod-103-review-standards-and-eval-commissioning/)'s eval commissioning and [`mod-106`](../mod-106-workflow-redesign-and-agent-delegation/)'s delegation playbook; author the team-scope incident retrospective so a reader two cycles later can act on it.

## Where this fits in the track

mod-109 sits between the *operational* modules (mod-103 review standards and evals, mod-106 workflow redesign) and the *governance* modules (mod-110 team-scope policy, mod-111 vendor governance). It is the module that turns the pinned accountability contract into practice when something actually goes wrong.

- **Back-links.** The human/machine contract drafted in [`mod-101`](../mod-101-ai-augmented-team-manager-position-and-contract/) Chapter 3 names the C3 accountability stack and the C4 "what happens when AI is wrong" flow. The review standards and the accountability contract from [`mod-103`](../mod-103-review-standards-and-eval-commissioning/) are the standards this module operates against. The task-routing matrix, delegation ladder, HITL checkpoints, and tool-authorisation policy from [`mod-106`](../mod-106-workflow-redesign-and-agent-delegation/) are the fault-chain layers Chapter 3 walks. The underperformance drill from [`mod-108` Chapter 5](../mod-108-hiring-performance-and-feedback-with-ai-in-the-loop/05-underperformance-root-cause-drill.md) is the parallel template that separates a *performance* conversation (manager / HR) from an *incident* response (this module) — the two must not be collapsed.
- **Forward links.** The policy registry in [`mod-110`](../mod-110-ai-governance-and-policy-at-team-scope/) folds this module's runbook and retrospective artifacts into the NIST AI RMF MANAGE 2.3 evidence base. The vendor-governance work in [`mod-111`](../mod-111-vendor-budget-and-tool-selection-at-team-scope/) draws on the incident retrospective when a vendor's SLA is in question. The ritual calendar in [`mod-112`](../mod-112-cross-functional-leadership-and-ritual-calendar/) is where the retrospective cadence and the regression-backfeed ritual land on the team calendar.
- **Sideways links.** [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) at level 25 (AI Governance family) owns engineered-risk root-cause depth — red-team reproductions, guardrail re-design, model-level evaluation. Managers commission and act on that work; they do not re-run it. SecOps / DFIR owns the SOC-side chain for incidents that involve data exposure, credential compromise, or intrusion.
- **Upward.** Cross-team incident coordination, regulator engagement, and public disclosure belong to [`ai-enablement-lead`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-enablement-lead-learning) (level 45) and above; [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (level 60) owns program-level incident reporting; [`chief-ai-officer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) (level 70) owns board- and regulator-facing communication. Chapter 7 names the upward handoff triggers.
