# Chapter 1 — The Governance Problem at Team Scope and Why This Module Exists

> *A team-scope manager will not write the organisation's AI policy. A team-scope manager will be the person who has to evidence, on a Tuesday, that the organisation's AI policy is actually in operation inside their team. That evidence is the deliverable of this module.*

## Motivation

The AI-governance literature is organised around organisational programs — board-level policy, program-level control libraries, enterprise-wide risk registers, audit-grade assurance. Owned by [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (level 60), architected by [`senior-ai-governance-architect`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning) (level 50), operated by [`ai-governance-analyst`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-governance-analyst-learning) (level 15), the program runs top-down.

Underneath that program, every actual team is where the controls are either real or theatre. The level-40 manager is the smallest unit of real AI-governance ownership: the person who signs off on what their ICs put into which agent, who schedules the AI-literacy training that satisfies [EU AI Act Article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), who runs the human-oversight checkpoint that satisfies [Article 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), and who produces the evidence that upward-rolling governance audits will actually read. The manager does not write policy; the manager *operates* policy and *evidences* operation.

That operational layer is poorly served by existing guidance. [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf) and the [NIST AI RMF Playbook](https://airc.nist.gov/AI_RMF_Knowledge_Base/Playbook) describe GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE functions at the altitude of the organisation; they are deliberately agnostic on how a specific team performs the subcategories. The [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) names obligations on deployers but does not prescribe team-scope training curricula. Microsoft's [Responsible AI Standard v2](https://blogs.microsoft.com/wp-content/uploads/prod/sites/5/2022/06/Microsoft-Responsible-AI-Standard-v2-General-Requirements-3.pdf) and [Google's AI Principles](https://ai.google/responsibility/principles/) describe program-level commitments; a team manager cannot run a program but can run the slice that a program inherits from them.

This module is the translation layer: taking published frameworks seriously (as published, not as straw-manned) and producing the team-scope artifacts that satisfy them in a way an upward audit will actually accept. The five artifacts land next to the artifacts from [`mod-103`](../mod-103-review-standards-and-eval-commissioning/) (review standards, accountability contract), [`mod-106`](../mod-106-workflow-redesign-and-agent-delegation/) (routing, HITL, tool-authorisation), and [`mod-109`](../mod-109-accountability-incident-response-and-blameless-postmortems/) (incident response). mod-110 is the one that turns the pile into a coherent governance packet.

This chapter does three things. First, it names what *team-scope governance* means and what it does not. Second, it names the five pieces of mod-110 and what they compose with. Third, it draws the scope boundary against the specialties around the manager: engineered risk sideways to `ai-risk-engineer`, architecture upward to `senior-ai-governance-architect`, program leadership upward to `head-of-ai-governance`, legal opinion out of scope to counsel.

## Core concepts

### What governance means at team scope

Governance at team scope is not the same thing as governance at program scope. Three practical differences shape every artifact in this module.

- **Scope of decision.** The manager does not set the organisation's position on, say, whether the team may use frontier models on customer data. The manager operates against the position counsel and the governance program have already set. The manager's governance decisions are the ones *inside* the policy envelope: *which* model under what policy, *which* data class may enter *which* tool, *which* HITL checkpoint applies to *which* workflow, *who* was trained to *what* level by *when*. These are the decisions an audit will check.
- **Scope of evidence.** The manager produces team-scope evidence: training records for the team, tool-authorisation decisions for the team, HITL-checkpoint logs for the team, incident retrospectives for the team. The manager does not aggregate across the organisation; that is the governance program's job. The evidence must be structured so that a program rollup can consume it without re-interviewing the manager.
- **Scope of liability.** The manager is not a regulated deployer *personally*; the organisation is. The manager is the operational owner of the subset of the deployer's obligation that lives inside the team. The manager flags questions that touch regulator-facing liability (notification deadlines, high-risk use-case classification, public disclosure); counsel decides them.

A useful shorthand: the manager owns **practice**, the architect owns **policy structure**, the head of governance owns **program accountability**, counsel owns **legal position**. Practice is runnable, logged, and evidence-producing. Policy structure is the control-library shape. Program accountability is what the regulator and the board see. Legal position is the formal interpretation of statutes. mod-110 is about practice.

### What this module is *not*

- **Not a legal-opinion course.** The EU AI Act, GDPR, state privacy statutes, sector-specific regulators (FDA, FCA, EEOC, HIPAA-covered-entity rules, etc.) are cited for context and used to scope obligations. The formal interpretation of whether a given workflow crosses a regulatory line belongs to counsel through the appropriate partner (General Counsel, Chief Privacy Officer, Data Protection Officer). The manager flags triggers, documents practice, and routes questions; counsel decides the legal position. Chapter 7 names the triggers.
- **Not an engineered-risk course.** Red-teaming, guardrail design, systematic model-level evaluation, and adversarial testing belong to [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (L25, AI Governance family) and, at frontier capability scope, [`agentic-safety-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/agentic-safety-engineer-learning) (L40). The manager *commissions* this work via the mod-103 eval-commissioning contract and *consumes* the evidence; the manager does not re-run the engineering.
- **Not a program-architecture course.** The design of the organisation's control library, the structure of the policy registry, the choice of governance tooling, and the alignment with [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html) belong to [`senior-ai-governance-architect`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning) (L50) and above. The manager is a *consumer* of the architecture and a *producer* of the evidence it needs.
- **Not a program-leadership course.** Board reporting, regulator engagement, public responsible-AI commitments, and cross-program governance accountability belong to [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (L60) and [`chief-ai-officer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) (L70). The manager contributes to these through the upward packet in Chapter 7, but does not author them.
- **Not a certification-prep course.** The [IAPP AIGP (AI Governance Professional)](https://iapp.org/certify/aigp/) body of knowledge is cited as context and overlaps with this module heavily, but preparing for the exam is a separate activity — see [`mod-112`](../mod-112-cross-functional-leadership-and-ritual-calendar/) for the certifications portfolio. mod-110 trains the practice; AIGP certifies general knowledge of the field.

## Example: the audit on Tuesday

A composite shaped example — calibration, not a case study.

A level-40 engineering-team manager at a mid-sized financial-services firm inherits a team that has been running Claude Code, Copilot, and a vendor-hosted RAG service across engineering and internal-ops workflows for three quarters. The firm's head of AI governance was hired two quarters ago; the governance program is young but real. The firm has a draft responsible-AI statement online referencing both the Microsoft Responsible AI Standard and the Google AI Principles; the firm is EU-headquartered so the AI Act applies; the organisation's legal team has concluded that a subset of the team's workflows (customer-facing underwriting assists, employment-screening automation touched by a sister team) are either high-risk or adjacent to it.

On a Tuesday morning, the head of AI governance asks the manager, by email, for the "team-scope AI governance evidence packet for Q4 — NIST RMF GOVERN subcategory coverage, Article 4 training records, Article 14 oversight evidence, Microsoft RAI goal coverage, data-handling policy, incident retrospectives." The regulator-facing deadline is two weeks away.

The manager realises, reading the email:

- The team has a pinned accountability contract (from mod-103) and an incident runbook (from mod-109). These are real artifacts but they do not map to NIST subcategory labels or Microsoft RAI goals. The audit will not accept "we do blameless post-mortems"; it will ask which NIST MANAGE subcategory that discharges and where the evidence lives.
- The team has been doing *some* AI training — a lunch-and-learn in July, a tool-rollout session in August, an incident retrospective in September that doubled as training. None of these are logged as "Article 4 AI-literacy training" or evidence-captured in a way the packet can consume.
- The team has a tool-authorisation policy (from mod-106). It is not written against the language of Article 14 human-oversight obligations, so when the auditor asks "how do you demonstrate effective human oversight on the underwriting workflow", the manager has to translate on the spot.
- The team has data-handling practice (what goes into which tool). It is partly written down (in the rollout plan from mod-102) and partly in Slack threads. Chapter 6 of this module is the artifact that collects it into one place.
- The upward packet itself does not exist as a template. The manager will build it this quarter; it is the foundation for every subsequent quarter.

The deliverable is not new policy — the firm already has policy. The deliverable is the *evidence that the team operates the policy*, in the shape the governance program consumes. That is mod-110.

## Summary

- Team-scope governance is **operational**, not programmatic: the manager runs the practice, logs the evidence, and routes the questions. Policy is set above; interpretation is held by counsel. Chapter 7 draws the lines precisely.
- The level-40 manager is **the smallest unit of real AI-governance ownership**: audits that do not read down to this layer are reading theatre. The upward packet in Chapter 7 is the interface the program consumes.
- **What is new under AI augmentation**: the trace that must be preserved (per [`mod-109`](../mod-109-accountability-incident-response-and-blameless-postmortems/)); the AI-literacy obligation named explicitly in statute ([EU AI Act Article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)); the human-oversight obligation attaching to specific workflow shapes ([Article 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)); the transparency obligation attaching to specific interaction shapes ([Article 50](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)); the data/IP question that attaches to every vendor data-use clause the organisation has signed.
- **Five artifacts over the module**: [NIST GOVERN crosswalk](exercises/exercise-01-nist-ai-rmf-govern-to-team-practice.md), [Article 4 training record](exercises/exercise-02-eu-ai-act-article-4-ai-literacy-for-your-team.md), [Microsoft RAI / Google AIP mapping](exercises/exercise-03-microsoft-rai-and-google-aip-team-mapping.md), [data/IP policy](exercises/exercise-04-data-and-ip-handling-policy.md), [upward evidence packet](exercises/exercise-05-upward-evidence-packet-for-governance-program.md).
- **Scope boundary**: not legal opinion (out of scope to counsel); not engineered risk (sideways to [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning)); not program architecture (upward to [`senior-ai-governance-architect`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning)); not program leadership (upward to [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) and above); not certification prep (see [`mod-112`](../mod-112-cross-functional-leadership-and-ritual-calendar/)).
- **Composes with** [`mod-101`](../mod-101-ai-augmented-team-manager-position-and-contract/) (C5 contract clause), [`mod-103`](../mod-103-review-standards-and-eval-commissioning/) (review standards, accountability), [`mod-105`](../mod-105-team-ai-enablement-programs/) (training program), [`mod-106`](../mod-106-workflow-redesign-and-agent-delegation/) (routing + HITL), [`mod-109`](../mod-109-accountability-incident-response-and-blameless-postmortems/) (incident retrospectives), [`mod-111`](../mod-111-vendor-budget-and-tool-selection-at-team-scope/) (vendor filter), and [`mod-112`](../mod-112-cross-functional-leadership-and-ritual-calendar/) (calendar).
