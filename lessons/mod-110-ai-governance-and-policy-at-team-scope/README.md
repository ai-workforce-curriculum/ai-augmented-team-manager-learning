# mod-110 — AI Governance, Policy, and Compliance at Team Scope

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours.

## What this module does

Turns the AI-governance program the organisation owns upstream (architected at level 50 by [`senior-ai-governance-architect`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning), led at level 60 by [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning), owned at level 70 by [`chief-ai-officer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning), interpreted legally by counsel) into **the operational practice and evidence shape the team manager is accountable for**. Translates the [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf) GOVERN function — using the [Playbook](https://airc.nist.gov/AI_RMF_Knowledge_Base/Playbook) as the day-to-day reference — into a team-scope crosswalk against which team artifacts (review checklists, routing matrices, incident runbooks) are mapped. Operates the [EU AI Act Article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) AI-literacy obligation on deployers as a sized, documented, dated training record at team scope. Where the team's AI touches regulated-use-case territory, operates [Article 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) human-oversight and [Article 50](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) transparency obligations through per-workflow sheets composed from existing [`mod-103`](../mod-103-review-standards-and-eval-commissioning/), [`mod-106`](../mod-106-workflow-redesign-and-agent-delegation/), and [`mod-109`](../mod-109-accountability-incident-response-and-blameless-postmortems/) artifacts. Produces defensive mappings against [Microsoft Responsible AI Standard v2](https://blogs.microsoft.com/wp-content/uploads/prod/sites/5/2022/06/Microsoft-Responsible-AI-Standard-v2-General-Requirements-3.pdf) and [Google AI Principles](https://ai.google/responsibility/principles/) that upward-rolling programs and customer questionnaires consume without restructuring team practice. Authors the team's data, IP, and confidentiality handling policy — the data-class × tool-class matrix, the vendor clause-reader, the per-model Hugging-Face-style [model-card](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/model-cards) record, the IP sidecar — that answers the Thursday-morning question about what data goes into which tool. Closes with the upward evidence packet, the single quarterly artifact that composes the above into the shape the governance program consumes.

This module is **not** a legal-opinion course — regulator engagement and formal interpretation of the AI Act, GDPR, and sector-specific statutes belong to counsel. It is **not** an engineered-risk course — adversarial testing, guardrail design, and model-level evaluation live at [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (L25, AI Governance family). It is **not** a program-architecture or program-leadership course — those live at [`senior-ai-governance-architect`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning) (L50) and [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (L60). The manager-scope deliverable is the *team's* operating practice and the evidence packet the program consumes.

## Learning objectives

- Translate the [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf) GOVERN function into team-scope practices — governance structures, accountability, roles, workforce — using the [NIST AI RMF Playbook](https://airc.nist.gov/AI_RMF_Knowledge_Base/Playbook) as the day-to-day reference.
- Meet the [EU AI Act Article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) AI-literacy obligation for deployers at team scope; follow the [EU AI Office](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-act) guidance to size, document, and evidence team AI-literacy training.
- Apply [Article 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) (human oversight) and [Article 50](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) (transparency) obligations where the team's AI touches regulated use cases; set team practice at the deployer tier.
- Map team practices against [Microsoft Responsible AI Standard v2](https://blogs.microsoft.com/wp-content/uploads/prod/sites/5/2022/06/Microsoft-Responsible-AI-Standard-v2-General-Requirements-3.pdf) and [Google AI Principles](https://ai.google/responsibility/principles/) — the two widely-cited industry frameworks used in public responsible-AI commitments; produce team-scope evidence for upward-rolling governance programs.
- Set data-handling, IP, and confidentiality practice for the team — what data goes into which tool, what leaves the premise, where vendor data-use clauses matter — composed with [Hugging Face model-card](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/model-cards) practice for team-selected models.
- Position the ownership boundary: engineering-grade risk eval lives at [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (L25, AI Governance family); program architecture at [`senior-ai-governance-architect`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/senior-ai-governance-architect-learning) (L50); program leadership at [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (L60); legal opinion out of scope to counsel.
