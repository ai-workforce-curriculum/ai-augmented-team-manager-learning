# Labs for mod-110-ai-governance-and-policy-at-team-scope (AI Governance, Policy, and Compliance at Team Scope)

The labs are guided practice on fixed, curated artifacts and scenarios for a shared fictional team (the "Claims Intelligence" team at Northstar Mutual — a 10-engineer team at an EU-headquartered personal-lines insurer running `ClaimsPilot` RAG and `UWCopilot` underwriting-assist). They **complement** the exercises (which ask you to author the artifact for *your own* team) by letting you calibrate your reading on deliberately composed cases before the stakes become real on your team's next quarterly packet, Article 14 triage, or Tuesday-afternoon IC decision.

Run order is suggested; each lab stands alone and uses the same fictional team so the team context is shared setup across all three.

- [Lab 01 — NIST GOVERN Crosswalk Cell Grading on a Composite Artifact List](lab-01-govern-crosswalk-cell-grading-on-a-composite-artifact-list.md) — 60–75 min. Grade twelve composite crosswalk entries for the fictional team against the Chapter 2 acceptance criteria (specific-URI citations + the Playbook's *Transparency and documentation* questions). Each entry is graded `evidence` / `wave` / `gap`, mapped to its primary GOVERN subcategory, and routed in the gap register (`upward` / `sideways` / `team-backlog` / `n/a`). Two entries are deliberately planted scope violations — a manager-authored risk threshold (violates Chapter 2's *"NIST intentionally does not prescribe"* silence) and a manager-drafted Annex III classification (violates Chapter 7's ownership-boundary line against practising law). Catches the two reading drifts Chapter 2 flags most often — grading a generic wave ("see the team wiki") as green and grading a well-authored scope violation as green because "the team did the work." Chapters: Chapter 2 primary; side-checks Chapter 1 scope and Chapter 7 gap-register routing. Complements [Exercise 01](../exercises/exercise-01-nist-ai-rmf-govern-to-team-practice.md).
- [Lab 02 — Article 14 and 50 Triage on Four Composite Workflows](lab-02-article-14-and-50-triage-on-four-composite-workflows.md) — 60–75 min. Triage four composite workflow proposals (AI-generated applicant-facing claim-decision letters; an internal engineering-wiki RAG; a voice-tonality "empathy signal" for call-centre coaching; the existing underwriting-assist agent extended to US and Canada) against the Chapter 4 reading of Articles 14 and 50, the eight-field workflow-sheet shape, and the five triggers for routing to counsel. Each workflow is classified (signal; counsel decides); the Article 14 sheet's composition plan is drafted (which fields inherit from mod-103 Ch. 5, mod-106 HITL, mod-107 anti-atrophy, mod-109 trace/routing); Article 50 sub-paragraphs (50(1), 50(3), 50(4), text-published-to-inform-the-public) are called; counsel triggers are fired with a bounded clean-handoff ask; and the "what the manager does *not* do" line is drawn for each. Catches the two reading drifts Chapter 4 flags — treating "internal-only" / "coaching-not-surveillance" framings as free passes from Article 50 when the workflow touches a natural person, and treating "high-risk-adjacent" or "same workflow, new geography" as grounds for the manager to interpret Annex III rather than route. Chapters: Chapter 4 primary; side-checks Chapter 1 scope boundary, Chapter 3 competence-training tie-in, Chapter 7 ownership boundary. Complements [Exercise 01](../exercises/exercise-01-nist-ai-rmf-govern-to-team-practice.md) (GOVERN 1.4 row) and [Exercise 05](../exercises/exercise-05-upward-evidence-packet-for-governance-program.md) (upward packet's Article 14/50 sections).
- [Lab 03 — Data-Matrix Decision Drill on Four Tuesday-Afternoon Scenarios](lab-03-data-matrix-decision-drill-on-four-tuesday-afternoon-scenarios.md) — 60–75 min. Given a pre-built Chapter 6 v1.3 data-class × tool-class matrix, a vendor clause-reader for Claude for Enterprise / Cursor Business / Consumer ChatGPT, a model-card index for four team-selected models, an IP sidecar, and the half-page data-leak trigger list, decide for each of four composite IC scenarios whether the action is allowed / conditional / prohibited and produce the 30-second response the manager would send back. Scenarios cover: the *enterprise-contract reflex* (IC thinks zero-retention + EU tenancy overrides a Restricted-class cell); the *anonymised paste* (IC strips obvious PII and assumes residual re-identification potential doesn't matter); the *query-shape* issue (handler asks a `ClaimsPilot` query with an applicant identifier in the query text, which matrix footnote ³ prohibits); and a *discovered paste* into Consumer ChatGPT that is now a data-leak event requiring incident-mode response (SecOps → counsel → preserve → do-not-contact-vendor). Catches the two reading drifts Chapter 6 flags — treating zero-retention as a free pass on a prohibited cell, and treating casual anonymisation as formal de-classification — and the one data-leak reflex ("I'll reach out to OpenAI to see if they can delete the chat") the trigger list's *"What not to do"* line is specifically designed to prevent. Chapters: Chapter 6 primary; side-checks Chapter 1 scope, Chapter 3 Module E, Chapter 7 data-leak-incidents field of the upward packet. Complements [Exercise 04](../exercises/exercise-04-data-and-ip-handling-policy.md).
