# Labs for mod-111-vendor-budget-and-tool-selection-at-team-scope (Vendor Selection, Tool Budget, and Build-vs-Buy at Team Scope)

The labs are guided practice on fixed, curated artifacts and scenarios for shared fictional teams. They **complement** the exercises (which ask you to author the artifact for *your own* team) by letting you calibrate your reading on deliberately composed cases — a bake-off results packet, a monthly usage snapshot, a set of vendor due-diligence packets — before the stakes become real on your team's next quarterly registry review, next finance-partner conversation, or next procurement submission.

Run order is suggested; each lab stands alone and introduces its own fictional team.

- [Lab 01 — Copilot Bake-off Scoring and Stance Assignment on a Pre-Baked Results Packet](lab-01-copilot-bake-off-scoring-and-stance-assignment.md) — 60–75 min. Score five pre-baked copilot candidates against the fictional "PayPilot" team's weights, apply the Axis 4 data-handling gate against an inherited `mod-110` Chapter 6 policy with PCI-scope restricted data, keep cost as a post-score threshold rather than folding into the score, assign a Chapter 3 stance (Buy / Wrap / Extend / route-upward) per candidate, and name an owner for every Wrap/Extend. Catches the two reading drifts Chapters 2 and 3 flag most often — *"Candidate B is cheap enough that its data-handling problem is manageable"* (gate-vs-weight) and *"Candidate D's extension surface is rich enough to justify Extend"* (unowned-extension). Also catches the proposed internal build routing (should route upward to `ai-enablement-lead` per R6 at 0.28). Chapters: Chapter 2 primary; Chapter 3 primary; side-checks Chapter 4 (authoring-environment overlap on Tier 3) and Chapter 1 (scope — routing upward is a legitimate output). Complements [Exercise 01](../exercises/exercise-01-copilot-build-vs-buy-matrix.md) and [Exercise 02](../exercises/exercise-02-agent-framework-selection-matrix.md).
- [Lab 02 — Stage-Shaped Budget Distribution Drill on a Composite Team Snapshot](lab-02-stage-shaped-budget-distribution-drill.md) — 60–75 min. Given the fictional "Harborlink" team's `mod-105` stage pins per tool, a current month's usage data (10 ICs with deliberately varied signals), pricing, and an org-level enterprise-commit shape, produce the three-layer budget distribution (seat, token, enterprise commit), a stage-shift projection for Q[N+1], a finance-partner narrative that composes with the `mod-104` AI-cost tile, and a monthly-review-signal pass. Catches the three reading drifts Chapter 5 flags most often — *"underuse is overspend"* on a stage-1 undershoot that is actually a `mod-105` programme signal; *"3x median is cap-worthy"* on a stage-4 engineer who is pattern-building; *"finance likes one number"* collapsing the three allocation layers into a line. Also catches the stranded-commit-share failure mode (team under-consuming the Claude for Work commit unflagged). Chapters: Chapter 5 primary; side-checks Chapter 1 scope (enterprise commits are `ai-enablement-lead`) and Chapter 7 (budget distribution as a registry input). Complements [Exercise 03](../exercises/exercise-03-tool-budget-and-token-budget-distribution.md).
- [Lab 03 — Due-Diligence Record Grading on Composite Vendor Packets](lab-03-due-diligence-record-grading-on-composite-vendor-packets.md) — 60–75 min. Grade four composite vendor packets (Cursor Business thin-draft; Claude Code as the Chapter 6 gold-standard shape; GitHub Copilot Enterprise with a stale SOC 2 Type I and a silent-model-routing clause; ClipMate Pro as a "cheap pilot" packet with multiple scope violations) against Chapter 6's seven sections, Chapter 7's off-cycle amendment triggers, and the ownership boundary. Each packet is graded `complete` / `gap` / `scope-violation` per section, routed to the correct partner (procurement / SecOps / legal), and admitted / conditionally admitted / rejected to the Chapter 7 registry. Catches the reading drifts Chapter 6 flags most often — *"same vendor, same DPA"* (tier-mismatched DPA on Packet 1), *"SOC 2 is SOC 2"* (Type I passing as adequate on Packet 3), *"model-provenance is nice-to-have"* (missing change-notice SLA on Packet 3 for an ITAR-adjacent team), *"exit plan is handled when we need it"* (asymmetric commitments with empty exit plans), and the two Chapter 7 ownership-boundary violations in Packet 4 (manager interpreting DPA § 4; manager drafting a vendor model-provenance commitment from a sales-call recollection). Chapters: Chapter 6 primary; Chapter 7 (ownership boundary, off-cycle triggers) primary; side-checks Chapter 1 scope. Complements [Exercise 04](../exercises/exercise-04-vendor-due-diligence-checklist.md) and [Exercise 05](../exercises/exercise-05-data-handling-dpa-and-soc2-posture-review.md).
