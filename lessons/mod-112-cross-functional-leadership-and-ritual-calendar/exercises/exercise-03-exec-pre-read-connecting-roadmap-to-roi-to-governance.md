# Exercise 03 — Exec Pre-Read Connecting Roadmap to ROI to Governance

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours.

## Objective

Produce **v0.1 of your team's quarterly exec pre-read**: a 4–8 page composed artifact that connects [`mod-102`](../../mod-102-team-ai-adoption-and-roadmap/) roadmap, [`mod-104`](../../mod-104-productivity-measurement-and-ai-roi/) productivity / AI-ROI, [`mod-109`](../../mod-109-accountability-incident-response-and-blameless-postmortems/) incident posture, [`mod-110`](../../mod-110-ai-governance-and-policy-at-team-scope/) governance evidence, and [`mod-111`](../../mod-111-vendor-budget-and-tool-selection-at-team-scope/) tool-registry evidence into one coherent narrative for your direct manager and the governance program consumer — with a single spine, one explicit ask, and reviewed honesty before leadership reads it.

The pre-read is the upward-composition layer of the team operating system (Chapter 7). The methodological anchor is [Chapter 4](../04-upward-communication-and-the-exec-pre-read.md) for the structure, the composition discipline, the board-adjacent / board-level boundary, and the cadence-and-delivery discipline.

## Problem statement

Leadership will decide something about your team this quarter — capacity allocation, strategic direction, tooling posture, governance stance — regardless of whether you have provided a pre-read. If the decision is made from your composed, honest pre-read, it will be a better decision than if it is made from a Slack thread, a half-remembered 1:1, or a dashboard URL without interpretation.

The problem the pre-read solves is composition. The team produces a lot of evidence (eleven backing artifacts); the VP reads *one* artifact per quarter. That one artifact must compose the relevant subset into a *single spine* — usually the roadmap-to-outcome arc, with productivity / ROI, governance / incidents, and vendor evidence in service of the spine. Four-separate-reports syndrome is the failure mode: four clean reports that leadership reads individually and combines incoherently.

The second problem the pre-read solves is honesty. A pre-read that inflates wins and softens gaps is not trusted after the second quarter it is read. The review discipline (PM, governance peer, sister-team peer manager) is the quality gate that keeps the document honest.

Scope: this is a **quarterly board-adjacent** pre-read at L40. Board-level narrative (annual, aggregated, firm-position, publicly committed) lives at [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) L60 and [`chief-ai-officer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) L70; this pre-read feeds *their* roll-ups, not the board directly. Writing in board register at L40 is over-reach.

## Steps

1. **Freeze the data.** Pull from the dashboard, the gap register, the tool registry, the incident index, the stage ladder, the roadmap. Use consistent period cut-off (end of fiscal or calendar quarter); everything in the pre-read is from the same frozen view. Do not re-pull mid-draft.
2. **Choose the spine.** Default: roadmap-to-outcome arc. Deviate only when the quarter was dominated by an incident (spine shifts to incident narrative), a markedly moved productivity signal (spine shifts to AI-ROI story), or a governance event (spine shifts to governance posture). The spine is one thing, not two. If you cannot choose a spine, re-run the draft discipline — the pre-read is not composed if it has no spine.
3. **Draft the executive summary (half page).** One-line claim at the top (the sentence the VP tells their manager in the hallway). Three to five material-fact bullets. Period covered, author, date, version. Named sign-off (manager; where joint, PM counterpart and governance peer).
4. **Draft the roadmap-to-outcome arc (one page).** What was committed; what landed; what slipped; what was swapped. The outcome-tile movement that corresponds. Not just activity volume — outcome. Cite the [`mod-102`](../../mod-102-team-ai-adoption-and-roadmap/) roadmap and the [`mod-104`](../../mod-104-productivity-measurement-and-ai-roi/) outcome tiles.
5. **Draft the productivity / AI-ROI narrative (one page).** Follow the [`mod-104` Ch. 7](../../mod-104-productivity-measurement-and-ai-roi/07-the-ai-roi-narrative-for-leadership.md) discipline. Headline DORA / DX Core 4 / flow / AI-cost tiles. What moved, what did not. Honest driver attribution (roadmap, enablement, tooling, external — not just AI). Anti-theatre paragraph from [`mod-104` Ch. 8](../../mod-104-productivity-measurement-and-ai-roi/08-qualitative-signal-against-activity-theatre.md).
6. **Draft the governance and incident posture (one page).** Count of incidents by [`mod-109`](../../mod-109-accountability-incident-response-and-blameless-postmortems/) class. Headline [`mod-110`](../../mod-110-ai-governance-and-policy-at-team-scope/) gap-register change: opened, closed, routed. Material policy / standard changes. Any routed-upward trigger with its state.
7. **Draft the vendor / tool-registry posture (half page).** Headline AI-spend; delta vs prior; projection. Registry adds / keeps / sunsets. Trial-status tools with deadlines.
8. **Draft the people and enablement section (half page).** Stage ladder distribution. Headcount changes. Growth-plan and anti-atrophy signal (team-level only, not individual). Hiring plans for next period.
9. **Draft the ask (half page).** One or two items, named, with a decision requested, a decision deadline, and the consequence of missing it. If nothing being asked, state so explicitly — a legitimate position. Validate the ask routes to the right Decide role per the Chapter 3 grid (Exercise 02).
10. **Draft the open loops and next period (half page).** Open loops carried forward with owner and expected resolution. Next period's roadmap at headline level. Next period's calendar changes (from Exercise 01 audit).
11. **Attach appendices (linked, not duplicated).** URLs to the eleven backing artifacts, decision log, ritual calendar, one-pager.
12. **Run the three-reviewer discipline.** Send the draft to the PM counterpart, the governance peer, and one sister-team peer manager. 48-hour turnaround. They review for honesty and shape, not for sign-off.
   - PM: roadmap / outcome honesty.
   - Governance peer: governance / incident honesty.
   - Sister-team peer manager: structural honesty — does the one-line claim support the detail; does it read to an outsider?
13. **Revise. Fix what reviewers flagged. Do not treat review as sign-off.** You own the pre-read.
14. **Deliver to direct manager 48 business hours before the leadership review.** Note the delivery in the decision log as a confirmation entry.
15. **Hold the VP office hour between delivery and review.** 20 minutes. Field push-back; prepare for the review itself. Record any concessions or clarifications; if material, log them.
16. **At the leadership review.** One slide, not the document. The document has been read; the review is for decisions on the asks and VP-flagged push-back items.
17. **Capture outcomes.** Record the decisions made at the review in the decision log. Flag the next-period pre-read's loop-closers.

## Starter guidance

- **The one-line claim at the top is the hardest sentence.** Write it last; write it ruthlessly. If you cannot write it in one sentence, the pre-read does not have a spine.
- **Honest driver attribution for productivity.** The AI-adoption curve is not the only driver of DX Core 4 movement; platform investment, roadmap composition, and enablement maturity all contribute. Honest attribution builds the quarter-over-quarter credit that lets the pre-read be trusted next quarter.
- **The anti-theatre paragraph is non-negotiable.** A pre-read that shows headline productivity gains without naming the qualitative-signal caveats will be read as marketing within two quarters. Keep the discipline.
- **Open loops have owners.** "Open loop: eval-regression backlog" without a named owner is a worry, not a loop. Name the owner even if the owner is you.
- **The ask section is for *one or two* items.** Three asks is an agenda, and the review slot cannot cover three asks. Prioritise.
- **No new decisions in a pre-read.** New decisions ambush the VP. Pre-reads formalise decisions that are already in discussion; they do not introduce them.
- **No personnel actions in the pre-read.** Individual performance and hiring decisions belong in 1:1 and performance-cycle channels. Team-level signals go in the pre-read; individual-level does not.
- **Attachments are links, not pasted content.** A 25-page pre-read is a structure that has failed. Link to the appendices.
- **Board register is over-reach.** "The firm's AI posture" at L40 is signalling, not substance. Use specific L40-register language: "the team has done X; the team has shipped Y."
- **Delivery timing matters.** A pre-read delivered 2 hours before the review is treated as attempted avoidance of scrutiny. 48 business hours is the floor; more is better.
- **Reviewer time is scarce — respect it.** Send the draft when it is ready for honest reading, not as a brainstorm input. A draft the manager would want to revise from scratch wastes reviewer cycles.

## Acceptance criteria

The pre-read is complete when:

- A single pre-read artifact exists at 4–8 pages with executive summary, roadmap-to-outcome, productivity / ROI, governance / incident, vendor / tool-registry, people / enablement, the ask, open loops / next period, and linked appendices.
- One spine is identified; all other sections are in service of the spine.
- The one-line claim is at the top; three to five material-fact bullets follow.
- Productivity section has honest driver attribution plus an anti-theatre paragraph.
- Governance section has incident count by class and gap-register delta with routed-upward state.
- Ask section has one or two items with decision requested, deadline, consequence of no decision; or an explicit "no asks this quarter."
- Three internal reviewers (PM counterpart, governance peer, sister-team peer manager) have read and returned comments; revisions are in.
- Delivered to direct manager at least 48 business hours before the leadership review.
- A VP office hour between delivery and review is held (or declined by the VP in writing).
- Outcomes from the review are captured in the decision log.
- A change-log entry names authors, reviewers, and date; v0.1.

## Stretch goals

- **Variance commentary.** For each headline tile, add one sentence of variance commentary: expected vs actual, with the explanatory factor. Converts "here is the number" into "here is the number and why it is what it is", which reduces leadership re-questioning.
- **Multi-quarter trend overlay.** The pre-read is usually read in isolation; a tiny trend sparkline per headline tile shows the current value in context without adding pages. Useful for VP who reads the pre-read quarterly and wants to see trajectory at a glance.
- **Previous quarter's ask-outcome review.** At the top of the current quarter's pre-read, note the outcomes of the previous quarter's asks. If an ask was granted and the promised outcome materialised, name it. This builds credit over quarters.
- **Pre-mortem the ask.** Before writing the ask, imagine the VP's three likely objections. Address them in the pre-read directly. A pre-emptively-addressed objection saves review minutes.
- **Confidence intervals on projections.** Next-period projections (headcount, cost, throughput) should have honest uncertainty ranges, not point estimates. "AI-cost projection: $X–Y, depending on Z" reads as serious; "$X exactly" reads as fictional.
- **Chapter 4 example as calibration.** Re-read the Chapter 4 worked pre-read example. Does your pre-read have the same shape — one spine, ask with deadline and consequence, three-reviewer discipline? Where it does not, understand why.
