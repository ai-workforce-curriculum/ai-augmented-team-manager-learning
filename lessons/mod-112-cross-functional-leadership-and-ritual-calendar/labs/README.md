# Labs for mod-112-cross-functional-leadership-and-ritual-calendar (Cross-Functional Leadership and the Team Ritual Calendar)

The labs are guided practice on fixed, curated artifacts and scenarios for shared fictional teams. They **complement** the exercises (which ask you to author the artifact for *your own* team) by letting you calibrate your reading on deliberately composed cases — a pre-baked fifteen-ritual calendar, three composite draft pre-reads, a one-pager plus five decision-log entries — before the stakes become real on your team's next annual calendar audit, next quarterly leadership review, or next operating-system retrospective.

Run order is suggested; each lab stands alone and introduces its own fictional team.

- [Lab 01 — Ritual Calendar Audit on a Composite Team's Fifteen-Ritual Calendar](lab-01-ritual-calendar-audit-on-a-composite-team.md) — 60–75 min. Grade the fictional "Riverline" team's fifteen-ritual calendar against Chapter 2: classify each ritual against Lencioni's four types (or overloaded), apply the tile-anchor and signal-of-decay discipline, compute per-ritual annual cost and calendar-budget %, make retire / right-size / split / keep decisions, and draft two Chapter 5 ADR-style decision-log entries for the two material retirements. Catches the three reading drifts Chapter 2 flags most often — *"a weekly team meeting that covers blockers, strategy, and open discussion is efficient"* (overloaded-slot-looks-like-efficiency), *"we don't need a defended tile for the ritual that's always been there"*, and *"the calendar doesn't actually cost that much because each meeting is short"*. Also catches the Chapter 1 / Chapter 2 scope violation — trying to retire the company all-hands the team does not own. Chapters: Chapter 2 primary; side-checks Chapter 1 (scope), Chapter 5 (decision-log template), Chapter 7 (calendar as layer 2 of the operating system). Complements [Exercise 01](../exercises/exercise-01-ritual-calendar-audit-and-refresh.md).
- [Lab 02 — Pre-Read Triage on Three Composite Draft Pre-Reads](lab-02-pre-read-triage-on-three-composite-drafts.md) — 60–75 min. Triage three deliberately-composed draft pre-reads — Draft A (Mapletone Logistics, four-spines-stapled-together); Draft B (Cambercroft Insurance, board-register over-reach with *"on behalf of the AI program"* signature); Draft C (Loomfield Retail, Chapter 4 model shape with a single roadmap-to-outcome spine, honest driver attribution, and a well-formed ask). For each draft produce a spine verdict, a register verdict, a composition check, an honesty-discipline check, an ask check, a delivery-discipline check, and a named fix. Catches the three reading drifts Chapter 4 flags most often — *"four clean sections is better than one spine with subordinate sections"* (four-separate-reports syndrome), *"board register signals seriousness"* (board-level over-reach at L40), and *"a dashboard URL in the appendix excuses a productivity section that is marketing voice"*. Also catches the two delivery-discipline failures: 24-hour delivery on Draft B (floor is 48 business hours) and the at-floor delivery on Draft A with no reviewer round. Chapters: Chapter 4 primary; side-checks Chapter 1 (scope), Chapter 3 (ask should route to a Decide role on the grid), Chapter 5 (one-pager honesty composes with pre-read honesty), Chapter 7 (L40 pre-read feeds L50/L60 roll-ups, does not try to be one). Complements [Exercise 03](../exercises/exercise-03-exec-pre-read-connecting-roadmap-to-roi-to-governance.md).
- [Lab 03 — Decision-Log and One-Pager Hygiene Grading on a Composite Operating-System Packet](lab-03-decision-log-and-one-pager-hygiene-grading.md) — 60–75 min. Grade the fictional "Dunfern Logistics, Rate-Quoting team" composite packet — a v0.3 one-pager and five decision-log entries. For the one-pager: grade each of Chapter 5's eleven sections and the three honesty disciplines (open-loops non-empty; *what the team is not* present; AI-ROI claims qualified). For the log: verify the ADR-style field set per entry (including reversal conditions and review date), distinguish complete vs weak vs scope-violation entries, and apply the template-scaling discipline in both directions. Catches the one-pager drifts Chapter 5 flags most often — *"a clean-looking one-pager is a trusted one-pager"*, *"specific numbers mean honest attribution"*, and the two missing sections (empty open loops; missing *what the team is not*). Catches the decision-log drifts — *"any decision in a log is better than no log"*, *"individual performance is team context so it belongs in the log"* (B4 scope violation: an IC PIP recorded in the team-visible log), *"missing reversal conditions is a minor omission"* (B2), and *"every entry needs the full ADR template"* (B1 over-templated trivial change). Also applies the Chapter 1 Monday-morning successor test to the composed packet. Chapters: Chapter 5 primary; side-checks Chapter 1 (successor test), Chapter 2 (ritual-calendar changes in the log), Chapter 3 (grid-change entries), Chapter 4 (the pre-read is quarterly; the one-pager is standing), Chapter 7 (operating-system retrospective reviews these artifacts). Complements [Exercise 04](../exercises/exercise-04-one-pager-and-decision-log-hygiene.md).
