# Lab 02 — Pre-Read Triage on Three Composite Draft Pre-Reads

**Duration:** 60–75 minutes.
**Chapters exercised:** Chapter 4 (the pre-read structure; one-spine composition discipline; board-adjacent vs board-level boundary; the ask section; review discipline; what the pre-read is not), with side-checks against Chapter 1 (scope — the pre-read is the upward-composition layer, not program narrative), Chapter 5 (one-pager honesty composes with pre-read honesty; the ask and open-loops sections mirror the one-pager's open-loops discipline), and Chapter 7 (ownership boundary — L40 pre-read feeds L50/L60 roll-ups and does not try to be one).
**Complements, does not duplicate:** [Exercise 03](../exercises/exercise-03-exec-pre-read-connecting-roadmap-to-roi-to-governance.md) asks you to author **your own team's** v0.1 quarterly pre-read — real roadmap, real tiles, real reviewers, real VP delivery. This lab hands you three deliberately-composed draft pre-reads from fictional teams so you can **calibrate your reviewer reflexes** — identifying the spine (or absence of it), spotting board-register over-reach, catching four-separate-reports syndrome, grading an ask for decision-deadline-consequence, and naming the fix per draft — before you spend three hours drafting your own pre-read whose first reviewer round surfaces a composition problem you had not learned to see.

## Objective

For each of three pre-baked draft pre-reads, produce a triage memo with: (i) a **spine verdict** (single named spine / four spines / mis-chosen spine / no spine); (ii) a **register verdict** (L40 board-adjacent / board-level over-reach / mixed); (iii) a **composition check** — do the other sections serve the spine, per Chapter 4's pattern (productivity reads the spine; governance protects it; vendor funds it; people populates it); (iv) an **honesty-discipline check** (anti-theatre paragraph present? honest driver attribution? open-loops populated with named owners? no personnel actions?); (v) an **ask check** (decision requested / deadline / consequence, routed to the right Decide role per the Chapter 3 grid, not a new decision ambushing the VP); (vi) a **delivery-discipline check** (48-hour floor; three internal reviewers; one slide not the document); and (vii) a **named fix** per draft — what the manager should change before next delivery, in one paragraph. Then compare against the answer key.

Target: **you identify the correct spine verdict on all three drafts, you catch the four deliberately-planted traps (four spines pretending to be one; board-register over-reach disguised as "executive voice"; a productivity section without an anti-theatre paragraph; an ask that is a new decision ambushing the VP), and your named fixes are specific enough that the drafting manager could execute them without re-reading the chapter.** The reviewer drifts this lab is designed to catch — *"four clean sections is better than one spine with subordinate sections"*, *"board register signals seriousness"*, and *"a dashboard URL in the appendix excuses a productivity section that is marketing voice"* — are the ones Chapter 4 flags as the most common first-pass review misses.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 4 read end-to-end — especially the nine-section structure, *"The composition discipline — connecting the four streams"*, *"The board-adjacent / board-level boundary"*, *"Cadence and delivery discipline"*, *"Review discipline before leadership sees it"*, and *"What the pre-read is not"*.
- Chapter 5 skimmed — the one-pager honesty disciplines (anti-theatre qualifier on AI-ROI claims; open-loops section non-empty).
- Chapter 3 skimmed — the decision-rights grid, since asks should route to Decide roles the grid names.
- Chapter 7 skimmed — the L40 ownership boundary against L50/L60/L70 narratives.
- A blank file for your triage memos.
- A timer.

Time yourself. Target: ~15–18 minutes per draft for Steps 2–3 combined; ~10 minutes for Step 4 comparison; ~5 minutes for Step 5 diagnosis.

### Step 2 — Triage Drafts A, B, C in order (40–50 min)

Work through each draft against the template. Do not look at the key until all three are done. Chapter 4's binding rules:

- *One spine per pre-read.* Four clean sections stapled together is not one pre-read.
- *Board-register at L40 is over-reach.* Specific L40 language ("the team shipped X") beats aggregated board language ("the firm's AI posture").
- *Anti-theatre paragraph is non-negotiable on productivity.* Headline gains without qualitative caveats will be read as marketing within two quarters.
- *The ask section is for one or two items with decision, deadline, consequence.* No asks is a legitimate position; new decisions ambushing the VP are not.
- *48 business hours is the floor.* Two hours before the review is attempted avoidance of scrutiny.

### Step 3 — Read the key and refine (10–15 min)

Read the key only after you have triaged all three drafts.

#### Answer key — Draft A (Mapletone Logistics, Freight-Routing)

- **(iv) Honesty-discipline check:** Anti-theatre paragraph present? **No** — the 18% DX Core 4 improvement is stated without driver attribution or qualitative caveat. This is the Chapter 4 failure mode named *"dashboards-as-narrative"* and the Chapter 5 failure mode named *"No AI-ROI headline number without the qualifier"*. Honest driver attribution? **No**. Open loops populated with owners? **No — empty.** Chapter 5 is explicit: *"Open loops section cannot be empty. A team with no open loops is either at an unusual moment of steady state (one quarter per year, if ever) or is hiding things."* The empty section is itself a trust signal. No personnel actions? **Y**.

### Step 4 — Diagnose your review drift (5–10 min)

Tally your verdicts against the key. For each miss, write one paragraph:

- **If you rated Draft A as having a spine (roadmap, or productivity, or "delivery") because the sections were individually clean:** this is the *four-clean-sections-look-like-one-pre-read* drift. Chapter 4's thesis is that composition is the pre-read's real work; four defensible sections stapled together is exactly the failure mode the composition discipline exists to prevent. The signal is in the executive summary: a pre-read with a spine has a one-line claim the VP can repeat in a hallway; a pre-read without has five parallel claims that do not combine.
- **If you rated Draft B's register as "appropriately executive" rather than board-level over-reach:** this is the *serious-language-looks-like-seriousness* drift. Chapter 4 is explicit that board-register at L40 is *noise* to leadership — simultaneously too coarse for the team's own operation and too fine for the board's attention. The sister-team peer manager's review question — *"does the pre-read make sense to someone who does not live inside the team?"* — surfaces this: the sister-team peer reads "the firm's AI posture" and has no idea what *team-scope* is being described.
- **If you rated Draft B's asks as legitimate because they name decisions:** this is the *decision-looks-like-ask* drift. Chapter 4 names asks as items the Chapter 3 grid predicts will land at a specific Decide role at a specific cadence; asks that escalate to the board or the firm route to roles the L40 pre-read has no interface with and therefore cannot be acted on from this pre-read.
- **If you rated Draft A's "no asks this quarter" as a problem:** this is reading Chapter 4 too narrowly. "No asks" is a legitimate position; the question is whether the surrounding pre-read supports the *steady-state* claim "no asks" implies. In Draft A, empty open-loops + four-spine composition + no anti-theatre paragraph *together* undermine the steady-state read; the "no asks" is not the problem on its own.
- **If you rated Draft C as having a composition problem because the productivity section is long:** this is the *brevity-looks-like-quality* drift. Chapter 4's productivity section is explicitly supposed to carry driver attribution and the anti-theatre paragraph; the length is the discipline, not the problem. Six pages for a quarterly pre-read is well inside Chapter 4's 4–8 page target.
- **If you missed the delivery-floor violation on Draft B:** this is the *review-the-content-not-the-cadence* drift. The 48-business-hour floor is a structural check that happens before the triage memo's other sections matter — a pre-read delivered 24 hours before the review is already in failure mode on delivery discipline, regardless of content.
